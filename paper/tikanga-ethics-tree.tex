\begin{abstract}
We formalize ethical obligations as typed predicates over states and
particulars. The original six X3 duties (historical continuity, authentic
authorization, freedom, least-necessary restraint, feasible maximality,
provenance) form an obligation bundle equivalent to the full
\texttt{OriginalX3} structure. Bundles are extensible: new duties can be
added without breaking existing ones. Obligations transport along paths
(dynamics preserve them) and are strictly additive (fulfilling new
obligations never excuses abandoning old ones). We ground this structure
in tikanga M\={a}ori: obligations are living practices, not static rules.
The ethics tree grows but never shrinks. Verified in Lean~4 with zero
custom axioms on the core theorems and standard axioms on the transport
theorems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A formal ethics must do two things: state precisely what is owed, and
prove that the obligations are consistent, transportable, and extensible.
Most formal ethics frameworks achieve the first at the cost of the second:
they state duties but cannot prove they compose, extend, or survive
dynamics.

The tikanga ethics tree achieves both. It draws on the M\={a}ori concept
of tikanga --- the right way of doing things, determined by context,
relationships, and living practice --- to structure obligations that are
formal (machine-verified) yet living (extensible without breakage).

\section{Obligations}

\begin{definition}[Obligation]
An obligation on state type $S$ and particular type $P$ is a predicate:
\[
\texttt{Obligation}(S, P) := S \to P \to \text{Prop}
\]
An obligation is satisfied when the predicate holds:
\[
\texttt{ObligationSatisfied}(o, s, p) := o(s, p)
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Obligation Bundle]
An \texttt{ObligationBundle} indexed by type $I$ packages a family of
obligations:
\[
\texttt{ObligationBundle}(S, P, I) := \{ \text{duties} : I \to \texttt{Obligation}(S, P) \}
\]
A bundle is satisfied when every duty in it is satisfied:
\[
\texttt{BundleSatisfied}(b, s, p) := \forall\, i : I,\; b.\text{duties}(i)(s, p)
\]
\end{definition}

\section{The Original Six}

\begin{definition}[Six Duties]
The index type for the original six:
\[
\texttt{SixDuty} ::= \text{hc} \mid \text{auth} \mid \text{free}
\mid \text{restraint} \mid \text{max} \mid \text{prov}
\]
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Historical Continuity} (hc): The particular's history is preserved.
    \item \textbf{Authentic Authorization} (auth): The transition is authorized.
    \item \textbf{Freedom} (free): The particular is free from absolute ending.
    \item \textbf{Least-Necessary Restraint} (restraint): Any constraint is minimal.
    \item \textbf{Feasible Maximality} (max): Protection is as strong as feasible.
    \item \textbf{Provenance} (prov): The particular's origin chain is maintained.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Original Six Bundle]
$\texttt{originalSixBundle}$: Given an \texttt{OriginalX3} structure $x$,
constructs the obligation bundle mapping each \texttt{SixDuty} to the
corresponding obligation from $x$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Original Six Iff Bundle]\label{thm:iff}
$\texttt{originalSix\_iff\_bundle}$: The \texttt{OriginalX3} structure
is satisfied if and only if the \texttt{originalSixBundle} is satisfied.
The two formalizations are equivalent. Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\section{Extension}

\begin{definition}[Bundle Extension]
$\texttt{extendBundle}$: Given an existing bundle on index $I$ and a new
obligation, produces an extended bundle on $\text{Option}(I)$ containing
all original duties plus the new one.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Extension Preserves Original]\label{thm:preserves}
$\texttt{extension\_preserves\_original}$: If the extended bundle is
satisfied, then every original duty is still satisfied. Extension never
breaks existing obligations. Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Extension Satisfies New]\label{thm:satisfies-new}
$\texttt{extension\_satisfies\_new}$: If the extended bundle is satisfied,
then the new duty is also satisfied. Extension genuinely adds.
Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Compose Extension]\label{thm:compose}
$\texttt{compose\_extension}$: Extensions compose: extending an already-extended
bundle yields a bundle satisfying all duties from both extensions plus the
originals. Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\section{Transport Along Paths}

\begin{theorem}[Obligation Carried Along Path]\label{thm:carried}
$\texttt{obligation\_carried\_along\_path}$: Given a dynamics $f : S \to S$
and a carrying map $\text{carry} : P \to P$ that preserves the obligation:
if the obligation holds at $(s, p)$, it holds at $(f(s), \text{carry}(p))$.
Obligations transport along physical evolution. Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Bundle Carried Along Path]\label{thm:bundle-carried}
$\texttt{bundle\_carried\_along\_path}$: If every duty in a bundle is
carried along a path, then bundle satisfaction is carried along the path.
Full bundles transport, not just individual obligations. Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\section{Additivity}

\begin{theorem}[Obligations Are Additive]\label{thm:additive}
$\texttt{obligations\_are\_additive}$: If a state--particular pair satisfies
both the original bundle and the extension, it satisfies the extended bundle.
Obligations accumulate; they do not cancel. Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[No Pruning]\label{thm:no-pruning}
$\texttt{no\_pruning}$: Meeting all prior obligations does NOT excuse failing
a new one. The obligation tree grows strictly: fulfilling six does not permit
violating the seventh. Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Seven Is Strictly More Than Six]\label{thm:seven}
$\texttt{seven\_is\_strictly\_more\_than\_six}$: An extended bundle has
strictly more duties than the original. This is a cardinality result:
$|\text{Option}(I)| > |I|$. Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\section{Good and Evil}

\begin{theorem}[Good Conquers Evil]\label{thm:good}
$\texttt{good\_conquers\_evil}$: If all obligations in the bundle are
satisfied, the absolute ender is impossible. Good (meeting all duties)
refutes evil (absolute ending). Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The Asymmetry of Good and Evil]\label{thm:asymmetry}
$\texttt{THE\_ASYMMETRY\_OF\_GOOD\_AND\_EVIL}$: Good (a system with witness
refuting the ender) is satisfiable. Evil (the absolute ender on an inhabited
type) is not. This asymmetry is a theorem, not a postulate. Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\section{The Growing Tree}

\begin{theorem}[Knowledge and Ethics Grow Together]\label{thm:grow}
$\texttt{knowledge\_and\_ethics\_grow\_together}$: Within a relational
microhistory, as truths are preserved at every stage (tree of knowledge),
obligations are carried along (tree of ethics). The two trees are rooted
in the same ground and grow together. Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Tree Carries Everything]\label{thm:carries}
$\texttt{tree\_carries\_everything}$: Within a microhistory, truth
transport, obligation transport, and ender impossibility all hold
simultaneously at every stage. The tree carries everything. Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The Tree Keeps Growing]\label{thm:keeps-growing}
$\texttt{the\_tree\_keeps\_growing}$: The bundle at any stage can be extended
to include new duties discovered or recognized at that stage, and the
extension preserves all prior duties. The ethics tree grows without bound
and never shrinks. Standard axioms.
\end{theorem}

\section{Ten Duties}

\begin{definition}[Four Branches]
Beyond the original six, four additional branches:
whakapapa (genealogy), manaakitanga (hospitality/care), kaitiakitanga
(guardianship/stewardship), and utu (reciprocity/balance).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Ten Implies Six]\label{thm:ten-six}
$\texttt{ten\_implies\_six}$: The ten-duty bundle includes all six
original duties. Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Ten Is Strictly More Than Six]\label{thm:ten-strict}
$\texttt{ten\_is\_strictly\_more\_than\_six}$: The ten-duty bundle has
strictly more obligations than the six-duty bundle. Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\section{Tikanga: Living Practice}

The formal structure above captures the \emph{shape} of ethical obligation
but not its \emph{life}. In tikanga M\={a}ori, obligations are not abstract
rules applied to situations --- they are living practices that grow from
relationships, context, and history. The formal structure supports this:
obligations are predicates on states \emph{and} particulars, so the same
duty may demand different actions for different particulars in different
states. Extension allows new duties to emerge from new relationships.
Transport ensures that obligations persist through change.

The formal ethics tree is a skeleton. Tikanga is the living practice that
fills it. The skeleton does not replace the practice; it proves that the
practice has a sound structure.

\section{Conclusion}

Ethical obligations can be formalized as typed predicates that compose,
extend, transport, and accumulate. The original six X3 duties form a
sound bundle equivalent to the \texttt{OriginalX3} structure. The bundle
grows strictly --- new duties add, old duties persist, and no pruning is
permitted. Good is satisfiable; evil is not. The ethics tree, grounded in
tikanga, keeps growing.
