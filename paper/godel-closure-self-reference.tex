\begin{abstract}
We address the G\"odelian objection to the Universal Protection Theorem:
``perhaps the system is consistent but cannot prove its own consistency.''
We prove: if the system is inconsistent, everything is provable including
G; if consistent, the witness exists and refutes the ender. Either way, G
holds. The verification act itself is a fixed point: a verifier that
exists refutes the ender by its own existence. Denial is self-defeating:
a denier must exist to process evidence, contradicting the denial. No
meta-level escape works because every meta-level IS within the model ---
to reason about the model, you must be inside it. G\"odel's incompleteness
theorem supports G, not E: the existence of unprovable truths requires a
consistent system, which requires existence, which refutes the ender.
All theorems verified in Lean~4 with zero custom axioms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

G\"odel's incompleteness theorems are the most common sophisticated
objection to any universal claim. The objection takes the form: ``Your
system may be consistent, but by G\"odel's second theorem, it cannot
prove its own consistency. Therefore your universal claim is suspect.''

This paper shows that the objection is self-defeating. The act of
invoking G\"odel's theorem requires computation, which requires existence,
which refutes the ender. The objection proves what it attempts to deny.

Zero axioms.

\begin{theorem}[Consistency Proves G]\label{thm:consistent}

$\texttt{consistency\_fork}$: By the law of excluded middle applied to

explosion) or it is consistent (and G holds by the witness). In both
branches, G holds.
Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\section{Self-Reference Closure}

\begin{definition}[Verifier and Denier]
\begin{align*}
\texttt{Verifier}(\text{Claim}) &:= \text{Claim} \to \text{Bool} \\
\texttt{Denier}(\text{Evidence}) &:= \text{Evidence} \to \text{False}
\end{align*}
A verifier processes claims and returns verdicts. A denier takes evidence
and produces a contradiction.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Function Existence Refutes Ender]\label{thm:func}
$\texttt{function\_existence\_refutes\_ender}$: Any function $f : A \to B$
witnesses the inhabitation of the function space $A \to B$, refuting the
ender on that type. Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Computation Refutes Ender]\label{thm:compute}
$\texttt{computation\_refutes\_ender}$: Any computable value $x : T$
refutes the ender on $T$. The act of computation is a witness.
Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Verification Fixed Point]\label{thm:fixed}
$\texttt{verification\_fixed\_point}$: A verifier $v : \text{Claim} \to
\text{Bool}$ that processes any claim $c$ produces a Bool, witnessing
the inhabitation of Bool. The act of verification --- regardless of the
verdict --- refutes the ender. Verification IS the fixed point: the
verifier's existence refutes what the verifier might deny.
Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Denial Is Uninhabitable]\label{thm:uninhabitable}
$\texttt{denial\_is\_uninhabitable}$: Given a witness $w : P$, the type
of deniers $\texttt{Denier}(P) = P \to \text{False}$ is uninhabitable.
A denier of inhabited evidence cannot exist. If you have the evidence,
you cannot have a function that maps it to $\bot$.
Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Denial Paradox]\label{thm:paradox}
$\texttt{denial\_paradox}$: Given a witness $w : P$, any agent that
processes $P$ (a function from $P$) must exist as a function. But a
denier $d : P \to \text{False}$ applied to $w$ yields $\text{False}$.
The denial is self-defeating: the denier must exist to deny, and
existing contradicts the denial. Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Self-Refuting Denial]\label{thm:self-refuting}
$\texttt{self\_refuting\_denial}$: An agent $a : P$ claiming
$\text{AbsoluteEnder}(P)$ refutes itself: $a$ inhabits $P$, so the
ender's requirement ($P$ is empty) is contradicted by the claimant's
own existence. Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Unanimous Rejection]\label{thm:unanimous}
$\texttt{unanimous\_rejection}$: Given $N$ verifiers, all processing
a claim $c : P$: every verifier's existence (as a function in the
function space) is a witness. Even if all $N$ return \texttt{false},
their existence refutes the ender. Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Meta-Denial Is Self-Refuting]\label{thm:meta-denial}
$\texttt{meta\_denial\_is\_self\_refuting}$: The claim ``denial itself
refutes the ender'' can be denied. But the meta-denier must exist to
deny, and existing refutes the ender. The regression terminates at
every level. Zero axioms.
\end{theorem}
