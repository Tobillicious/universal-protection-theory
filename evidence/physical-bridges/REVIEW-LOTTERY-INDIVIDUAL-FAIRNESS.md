# Referee check — outcome-uniform versus individual-fair tragic-choice lotteries

This independent source-level Lean witness addresses a specific
risk in the original book's tragic-choice/collective-coordination
discussion. Under the **unchanged UPT lexicographic retention order**
on all feasible outcomes, take four maximal choices over five
protected particulars:

- 0 keeps {0,1}
- 1 keeps {0,2}
- 2 keeps {1,2}
- 3 keeps {3,4}

Every distinct choice is incomparable by strict inclusion, hence all
four are original-order maximal. A lottery uniform over the **four
outcomes** yields marginal retention chances `(2,2,2,1,1)/4`
for the five particulars. Thus treating each *outcome* equally
does not automatically give each *individual* equal chances.

A different ticket weighting `[0,1,2,3,3]` produces marginal
retention probabilities `(2,2,2,2,2)/5`; this equalizes
individual marginal chances without modifying feasibility or X3.

**Crucial limitation:** equal individual marginal chances are
neither asserted to be the unique fairness rule, nor guaranteed
achievable in every feasible family. Nonuniform lottery weights can
alter other ethical priorities; additional compatibility/consent/
anti-manipulation premises may matter. A five-ticket synthetic model
does not establish real-world implementation or protection.
