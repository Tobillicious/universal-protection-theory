# IR-80 — Stored-energy, peak-transient, common-mode and fail-closed controls

**Source provenance:** Gemini adversarial hardware-security review concerning inductive kickback `E_L = L I²/2`, inter-sample blindness, shared ADC references, watchdog SEU/brownout and the proposed continuous-time residual displacement theorem. This development retains the original X3 target and IR-79 physical application-time effect margin without claiming an actual hardware instrument.

## Direct source-level theorems

1. **Correct electrical energy dimension:** The ideal inductor's magnetic energy is `E_L = L*I²/2` (joules). We work in `2E` integer units, `2E_L = L*I²`, to make the rational factor explicit while keeping a small exact Nat proof. **Integral of current over time is electric charge in coulombs**, not joules. Energy transfer from inductive flyback is governed by voltage-current work such as `∫ V(t)*I(t) dt`, subject to sign conventions, and circuit dynamics such as `V = L dI/dt` under the ideal-inductor approximation.
2. **Physical energy ledger:** Given the independently warranted accounting premise `storedTwice ≤ absorbedTwice + escapedTwice + remainingTwice` and the clamp rating `absorbedTwice ≤ clampCapacityTwice`, if no energy escapes and no stored energy remains, **the clamp capacity must cover the entire magnetic + kinetic + other stored energy**, not just the field energy. Contrapositively, an undersized clamp forces either a nonzero escape channel or remaining stored energy under the stated ledger. No measurement or physical completeness is conjured by this theorem.
3. **Energy versus peak voltage:** A synthetic source-level countermodel has sufficient absorbed energy capacity while peak voltage exceeds an independent safe-voltage threshold. **Energy capacity alone is not a peak-voltage, thermal or mechanical safety certificate.** The actual circuit's capacitance, switching derivative, energy paths, clamp voltage, insulation/stress tolerance and thermal evacuation each need separately verified assumptions.
4. **Common-mode sensing:** Two sensors can agree exactly above a safe recorded threshold while true physical energy is below the reserve. A positive conditional result is also proved: if **at least one** channel has a genuine externally grounded one-sided error envelope, and both reported values are above their individual padded thresholds, the true physical reserve follows. Correlated agreement is NOT evidence of the at-least-one-sound premise.
5. **Watchdog and fence readiness:** a modeled conjunction of telemetry pass, analog-watchdog readiness and final physical fence readiness is fail-closed if either latter boolean input is false. A source-level bypass counterexample permits a physical effect even when all logical permissions pass. Merely deploying two watchdogs does not establish independence from shared power, shared reference, common firmware/clock, or shared actuator wiring.
6. **Inter-sample hazard:** The existing Lean-checked IR-52 example is imported and repackaged to prove that perfectly safe every-second samples can conceal a dangerous pulse between them. No universal Nyquist-rate hazard protection follows from sampling unless the signal is appropriately band-limited or the physical hazard evolution is bounded by a separately justified envelope. Hazard-control proof requires the latter, not a reference to Nyquist alone.

## Problems in Gemini's proposed continuous-time proof

- It says disturbances are “unbounded” while simultaneously postulating `||d(t)|| ≤ Dmax`, a finite disturbance cap.
- The claimed residual model `u(t)=u_active*max(0,1-(t-t_fault)/δ)+Flyback(t)` is a *chosen actuator waveform*, not a consequence of the BCRD dynamics or a general inductor model. It omits circuit saturation, back-EMF, nonlinear switches, mechanical inertia coupling and recovery channels.
- The suggested substitution `(E_flyback/L)*exp(-t/τ)` into a bound on `||f(0,u(t))||` is dimensionally unjustified: `E/L` has units of **current squared**, not force or plant-state velocity.
- Its scalar inequality `x_crit - x(t_fault) ≥ ||Δx|| + epsilon` is not meaningful for arbitrary `x∈R^n` without a specified scalar safety barrier/distance function, norm-compatible gain and direction/geometry of the unsafe set.
- Grönwall's inequality does not provide a finite bound on `||f(0,u)||` until Lipschitz/growth constants, input envelopes and integrability are actually supplied. The supposed trigger lookahead “incorporated in IR-77” was not established; IR-77 checks completed *recorded* finite traces rather than forecasting future catastrophic trajectories.
- The use of ≥ for the clearance condition yields at most a non-strict endpoint bound ≤ unless a strict safety margin is independently provided. An initially breached fault cannot be undone by the detection delay theorem.

## Physical bridge still OPEN

To claim a real-world result requires a complete *actual* plant model, calibrated stored energy and hazardous physical effects, true fall-back paths for arbitrary faults, microsecond/microstep or continuous-time dynamics, authenticated and independently calibrated sensors under correlated faults, detection delay and actuator-fence latency, complete stored-energy dissipation/in-flight work, all causal bypass channels and a protective margin present **before** the physical breach. It further requires the original X3 positive history identity, service, autonomy and priority obligations; an absorbing forever-off latch cannot be assumed to satisfy these.

The source is a conditional energy-accounting theorem and hostile logical controls, not a physical thermodynamics or electrical-engineering certificate. Real-world failures such as EMI, capacitor rupture, thermal runaway and high-power switching require qualified engineering review and safe test procedures; these proofs are not authorization to perform destructive tests.
