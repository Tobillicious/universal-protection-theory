## GHOST-HAZARD-03 — positive end risk can coexist with indefinite survival

With per-generation conditional ending risk \(q_n\), the probability of surviving the first N transitions is \(\prod_{n=1}^N(1-q_n)\) **when each \(q_n\) is defined conditionally on prior survival**. No independence assumption is needed for this chain rule.

If \(q_n\ge\varepsilon>0\) uniformly, the previous INFINITE-ANCESTRY-01 result gives survival probability at most \((1-\varepsilon)^N\to0\).

**Exact positive-risk countermodel:** take \(q_n=1/(n+1)^2\), for \(n=1,2,\ldots\). Every generation has strictly positive risk, yet

\[
\prod_{n=1}^N\left(1-\frac1{(n+1)^2}\right)
=\frac{N+2}{2(N+1)}
\longrightarrow \frac12.
\]

**Proof:** each factor \((1-1/k^2)=(k-1)(k+1)/k^2\); the two products telescope exactly. Thus a model can allow end events at every generation and still give positive probability to surviving an endless sequence. Indefinite survival **does not force every ending risk to be zero**, and a positive-probability surviving lineage does not uniquely identify guardian architecture.

The exact proof sharpens the old K2/DOOM “cannot win” discussion: whether risk is bounded below or decays, and whether an “ender” is a mind, failed attempt or *successful total lineage-ending event*, are decisive. None is independently derived here from physical BCRD cosmogenesis.

## GHOST-DENSITY-04 — preserve old G, do not transfer its growth premise blindly

The **legacy Theorem G** used growing keepers and bounded concurrent enders/harmful episodes. In an elementary continuous population model, if
\(E(t)\le a\) and \(K(t)\ge bt\) for \(t>0\) with \(a,b>0\), then

\[
\frac1T\int_0^T \frac{E(t)}{E(t)+K(t)}\,dt
\le \frac{a}{bT}\log\left(1+\frac{bT}{a}\right)
=O\left(\frac{\log T}{T}\right).
\]

This is a **reapplication of the old density argument**, not new proof of its population assumptions. In stochastic systems, a bound on **expected** ender occupancy alone does not automatically give a pathwise bound on the random fraction; keep expected and pathwise quantities separate.
