## 2. Namespaces: never confuse these premises

`O1–O4` are the **four Given the Lattice / BCRD-working ontological properties** (S01 PDF pp. 3–4):

- **O1:** one relational structure; no separate substance or external standpoint.
- **O2:** relational identity of physical systems.
- **O3:** real differentiated, persistent, historically continuous particulars.
- **O4:** local effects propagate at finite speed.

`U-P1`, `U-P2`, `U-P2′`, `U-P3`, `U-P4`, `MR`, `IS`, `EDC` are **separate engineering/game/physical assumptions** in the 18 Oct UPT-P (S05, §3). For example, `U-P3` *assumes bounded reach-or-hold coverage*; `U-P4c` *assumes* adequate keeper capacity/headroom. Neither follows by symbol similarity from O3/O4. The 19 Oct `M1–M3`, `C-PHYS`, `C-SEQ`, `JW` design parameters are a third namespace (S06–S07). This is a major source of potential circular reasoning.

`O1–O4` are working premises for internal conditional research, not an empirical claim that their stronger BCRD origin has been validated. Even if they are granted, a moral rule or a particular physical protector does not follow without bridge premises.

## 6. New conditional no-go: finite-speed locality exposes the external-reach obligation

**Statement (logical non-entailment from O1–O4 alone).** The class of locally updating, finite-speed relational worlds with historically continuing subjects contains models where an external guardian cannot prevent an immediate irreversible destructive action. Thus **O1–O4 alone do not logically entail a universal external-protection guarantee**. This does not refute stronger BCRD premises not represented by O1–O4.

**Explicit countermodel.** Take a finite connected graph with a continuing subject at vertex `s`, a local attacker at adjacent vertex `a`, and a guardian at vertex `g` at graph distance > 2 from `s`. Subject state has `alive ∈ {0,1}`, initially 1, and its past relations encode a genuine lineage. At step `t=0` the attacker may set a local `attack` bit. The allowed radius-1 transition makes `alive(s,1) = alive(s,0) AND NOT attack(a,0)`. No shield or veto is initially installed at `s` or `a`. The guardian's commands cannot propagate to the transition at `s` in one step. All updates occur on one graph with relationally specified histories and radius-1 propagation, so the model has O1–O4 in their broad form. The attacker chooses `attack=1`; the subject's continuation ends before the distant guardian can intervene. ∎

**Necessary intercept alternative (conditional).** To prevent *that* loss in *that* state, a protector must either (i) alter the admissible local dynamics so destructive action is physically barred, or (ii) place an effective veto/interlock in the causal past of the vulnerable transition. This is a **necessity for the local countermodel**, not a proof that every possible BCRD world has an interlock or a protector. If the uncontrolled local attack has probability `p>0` under an allowed adversarial/noise law, then in the countermodel global safe-continuation probability is at most `1−p`, regardless of distant guardian power. The numbers and graph are deliberately abstract; no BCRD laboratory fact is asserted.

**Implication for the architecture.** The outer reach theorem must certify a **causal-interception condition**, not merely an eventual discovery rule. A mind can be “reached” too late to prevent a loss. For each protected class `i` and destructive event `e` with deadline `d_e`, show either a BCRD-derived prohibition of `e` or an already-available, authorized protector action in the relevant causal predecessor set, with service/deadline budget intact. Birth/admission and new external types must be included.

## 7. Candidate *joint* UPT statement (NOT YET AN ESTABLISHED THEOREM)

Fix a single BCRD-compatible transition system `S` with time, states and (where stochastic) one filtered probability space. Let `I` be a typed protection invariant: historically continuing subjects, direction, peers, resources, deadlines, lawful consent/authorization and no wrongful extinction. Let `E0` be initialization/founding failure, and `F_(t,k)` the **first** observable failure attributable to channel `k` at time `t` while earlier states remain safe.

**Sufficient premises to prove from one S:**

- `J0`: The initial joint protected state is nonempty **and reachable in time from the specified initial condition**; failure `Pr(E0) ≤ eps0` explicitly charged.
- `J1`: All relevant subjects/events are covered; class extension satisfies a frame/resource condition, with residual uncovered event `U` measured/bounded instead of renamed away.
- `J2`: All actually executable protective transitions preserve every applicable type of `I`, including authorization and peer effects.
- `J3`: Guardian funding, stocks, work, controllers, host capacities, network paths and latencies are simultaneously feasible *on the same trace*; full loss-of-service envelopes specified.
- `J4`: Gate B/E/D conditions hold for participating external agents OR the physical plant resists their actions by an independently established invariant; never infer one from the other.
- `J5`: All admissible destructive events are either prohibited by proven dynamics or causally interceptable before their deadlines.
- `J6`: For each reachable safe history, `Pr(F_(t,k) | history) ≤ eps_(t,k)`; separate common shocks, model misspecification and unrepresented faults, and show `sum eps_(t,k) < ∞` where an infinite horizon is claimed.

**Then**, by induction on the first violation and the countable union bound (the G1 argument already present in S10 §3),

`Pr(I holds through the stated horizon) >= 1 − eps0 − Pr(U) − Σ_(t,k) eps_(t,k)`.

This **conditional implication is mathematically elementary once its premises are truly met**. The hard and currently open claim is that `J0–J6` can all be derived or independently witnessed in *one* physically permissible system, especially beyond a fixed jurisdiction. No strong version should call `J0` or `J1` proven by smuggling “reach-or-hold” into its definition. If `Pr(U)` cannot be estimated, leave it unbounded.

### Minimal countermodel tests for proposed joins

- **Early unreachability:** radius-1 local attacker wins before distant keeper's signal arrives (above).
- **Shared resource:** A and B each require one service unit; a plant with only one cannot provide both simultaneously. New class protection is not monotone without a frame/capacity proof.
- **Common shock:** All `n` keepers fail on a Bernoulli shock of probability `.01`. Failure remains `.01` as `n` grows; independence/mixing assumptions matter.
- **Consent spoofing:** A target's state causally participates in the effect because an external attacker forced it; causal origin does not establish authorization.
- **Archive substitution:** A log preserves a prefix but the original external subject dies; `H_process` cannot discharge `H_external`.
- **Initial capacity:** `r<1` in NP despite `C>H`; splitting alone does not provide a universal guard.
- **Twilight:** `T*_η<T_end` despite non-rival keep-policy dominance; policy preference does not guarantee survival.
