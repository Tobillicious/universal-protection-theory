## The prior three objections, corrected

**(1) "Partial doesn't mean false."** Correct about a local experience as an actual relation. It is *irrelevant as an objection* to the claim that a partial description cannot determine the totality. A cut may be truthful about its observations while unable to determine the source fact. Joined A and B demonstrate exactly that.

**(2) "An eternal whole can contain finite processes."** A bounded local record does not erase its events from the whole and does not constitute a globally annihilating operation. That **invalidates an alleged inference from a cut-local disappearance to absolute nothingness**. However the original V.2 explicitly defines loss of a subject's future self-directed history as a meaningful (and generally prohibited) ending *inside* the whole. The mathematical whole containing that history does **not itself derive** the stronger full-X3 continuation. Do not silently redefine the author's original X3 protection to mere occurrence in a complete historical record. IR128/IR102 provide genuine *conditional physical* preservation routes.

**(3) "Something does not imply every conceivable thing."** The author's statement means the one *complete actuality* is there if any of its events is there, not that all mutually inconsistent mathematical objects and imagined outcomes are actual. Formal reading: `e∈E_actual ⇒ E_actual ≠ ∅`, and `E_actual` is the totality of actual relations, not `all propositions are true`. Thus the modal objection "not every imaginable object exists" does not contradict the intended thesis. Conversely, the argument must not use "EVERYTHING" to smuggle in every feasible future or every original-X3 protection.
