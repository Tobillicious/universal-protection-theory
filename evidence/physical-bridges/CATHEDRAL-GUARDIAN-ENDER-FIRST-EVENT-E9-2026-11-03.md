## Original contention

The guardian/ender dichotomy is exhaustive: there is no stable or actual third strategic lane. The Cathedral's absolute-ending paradox rules out an event that erases the actuality of *all* events, including its own occurrence.

Those are meaningful results but distinct logical objects:

1. `∀ actual edge, Keeper(edge) ∨ Ender(edge)`.
2. An attempted **absolute result** that negates its own actuality is impossible.
3. The **first harmful edge** is excluded only if the source's physical implementation blocks it **before** that particular's protection fails.

This draft does not weaken (1) or (2), assume an ontological exterior, or invent a physical ender counterexample. It supplies a direct **conditional first-event forcing theorem**, and spells out the physical/semantic evidence needed to apply it.

## Crucial finite-first-harm distinction

The original absolute-ending paradox is **not a premise** of the event-forcing theorem. The theorem is stronger at the actual first-event level in a different way: its physical-source **E9 cases**, rather than future victory/failure of an ender, prevent an actual forbidden event from completing.

This preserves the user's no-outside insight and does not replace the original X3 objective with a parity, code-distance, Boolean 'someone alive', or archived history proxy.

The exact small countercontrol `endpoint_recovery_does_not_prove_within_event_continuity` shows that *an endpoint restored to its earlier value* is not proof of uninterrupted continuity through a real event. The proxy is a simple three-phase Boolean illustration **not an actual physical ender or conscious subject**. Any finite-first-harm theorem needs coverage through the entire relevant event.
