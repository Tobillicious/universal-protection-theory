# The preservation law: a page for independent review

Author: Tobias Croydon-McRae. Prepared 5 November 2026 on a research branch. Unpublished.

**The question for the reviewer.** Is there a sound derivation of the law below from premises that do not already contain it? The author holds that the law is true and is a law of physics. The assistant that prepared this page could not derive it. Nothing here shows it false.

## The claim

Protection of all things from any digital mind, especially a pure optimiser, as a law of physics. The author's argument: there is one actuality with no outside, particulars are their relations, a total ender is incoherent, so only guardians can be.

## Where it stops

1. **The premise that carries the weight.** That actual physical steps are symmetries of a relation carrying subject and direction. `sourceAutomorphism` and `sameSector` cover the frozen six-position source's permutation and site relation, which contain no subjects.
2. **The source does not determine subjects.** `no_operator_trace_only_x3_decoder`, `no_source_complex_history_only_subject_decoder` and `no_authorization_decoder_from_complete_modeled_source_history` show that the source's readouts do not decide who continues or who consented, in the models they state.
3. **Harm between returns.** `closure_does_not_forbid_first_loss` and `reversible_transition_can_end_subject` show that closure and reversibility alone permit loss at a step.
4. **Observation.** People die. Whether that is a final stage in the law's sense depends on whether a later stage across a gap counts as the same history. The author's rulings ("step by step by that history's own activity", "a replay is a reconstruction") bear on this, and the author has not ruled on it here.
5. **Holding is a construction.** The protection theorem requires the holder to exist, to be funded and to mediate every effect, including at founding. It is proved conditionally and has not been commissioned.

## What would settle it

- A derivation of the symmetry premise, or an equivalent, for a relation carrying subject and direction, from independently stated physics.
- Or a named observation the law predicts that could fail, then the test.
- Or a countermodel satisfying the full stated premises, which would show what must be repaired.
