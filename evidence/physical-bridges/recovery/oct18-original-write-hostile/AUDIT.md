## Refutation A — Gate C-infinity necessity needs temporal support

The fixed-rate advance-bill perpetuity fixed point is e*=4(1+r)/r. If return r_t>=r_min>0 along **all paths** and e_0>=4(1+r_min)/r_min, the source's sufficient funding guarantee is correct.

The source also claims an **essential infimum** r_min implies that starting below that worst-case price gives failure with positive probability, and that a positive chance of nonpositive returns implies no finite safe endowment. Neither follows from per-epoch support alone; one needs jointly possible *arbitrarily long adverse runs*.

Counterexamples with exact fractions, bill 4 and e0=50:
- r0=4%, all later rt=10%. Initial capital 50 is BELOW the 4% worst-case price 104, but after one epoch balance is 47.84, exceeding the 10%-forever safe threshold 44. All future bills are funded indefinitely.
- r0=0 (positive probability 1), all later rt=10%. Next balance is 46>44, again safely funding all future care despite a nonpositive-return epoch.

The explicit checker preserves the source's sufficient positive-floor result, refutes the two broad necessity statements, and checks that arbitrarily long zero/4%-return runs can indeed ruin the same initial capital. A sufficient repair is an explicit positive-probability support premise for sufficiently long near-minimum or nonpositive RETURN SEQUENCES, not merely a per-date marginal possibility. This is a genuine mathematical correction, not an argument that universal physical protection has been achieved.

## Original ALL-bridge qualifications

1. The 22 October Unconditional Protection Theorem U1 follows from **an already-selected inclusion-maximal feasible outcome**. It does not establish that arbitrary infinite feasible families contain such a maximal choice. Counterexample: the feasible outcomes are all finite subsets of the natural numbers, each strictly extendible. IR-56/66–72 address selected infinite cases with additional assumptions. The label "no premises" cannot supply an absent existence proof.
2. F1 Bridge-to-ALL v0.3 treats preserving a historical record after physical ending as protecting that particular. A record is a **different particular**; retaining it does not automatically continue the ended original. The later Part II bridge says "record and continuation" but has not independently proved the physical identity interface.
3. Part II E7 says a perfectly faithful hold "strictly dominates" standing by and striking at *every* threat probability p. At p=0 (no threat) the perfect hold and standing by preserve the same histories, so strict dominance over standing by fails; weak dominance holds. At other p the theorem still depends on ideal, fully reversible, timely, noncoercive, physically executable holding. The strictness phrase and the real physical assumption must both be qualified.
