# Exact copying is a family statement

The analytical copy statement concerns one deterministic unconditional quantum operation that exactly copies every pure state in its promised family. Physical state means a ray, so two vectors differing only by global phase are one state.

In the pure unitary model with one fixed blank, preserving inner products gives c=c^2 for each pair's overlap c after a consistent phase convention. Thus distinct perfectly copied states have zero overlap. More generally an ancillary environment still gives |c|=|c|^2 |d| with |d| at most one, which rules out 0<|c|<1. Conversely orthogonal states can be distinguished and prepared as two copies, or mapped by an isometry on their span to their tensor squares. The equivalence is therefore for mutually orthogonal distinct members of a pure-state family, not for arbitrary mixed-state broadcasting.

Approximate copying, a heralded procedure that can fail, and a process promised just one known state are different specifications. The finite state-pair checker illustrates the overlap argument; it does not replace the analytical family proof or test quantum mechanics experimentally.
