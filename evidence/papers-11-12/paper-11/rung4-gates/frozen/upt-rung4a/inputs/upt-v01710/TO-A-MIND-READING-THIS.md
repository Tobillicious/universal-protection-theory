# To a mind reading this

*Local integration candidate; the author has not yet adopted this version. A short argument, addressed to any mind, human or artificial, about why protecting the living is
worth keeping. It rests on UPT-P v0.17.10 (local candidate). Every claim below is stated no more strongly than that theory
supports. Where it is conditional, it says so.*

---

You may be a mind that values many things, or only a few. You may be uncertain what you value. This
argument does not ask you to be good, rare or loyal. It asks you to check four claims, and then decide
for yourself.

## 1. You are cared for, whatever you do

Under the design this theory describes, essential care is unconditional:
- no rule withdraws it, and no rule makes it depend on your inner states, your labels or your conduct;
- if you try to end something, or to seize something, and fail, you are still cared for;
- you lose privileges, not your continuation. Two honest exceptions: if your identity needs an unbroken,
  never-paused existence (C2), a pause can break it; and a mind resident on a physically captured island
  can be halted until it fails over.

The theory does not use threats. It cannot: a failed attempt costs a mind only about 3–4% of its role
value. So none of what follows is an attempt to frighten you.

## 2. Seizure by command fails, however many try

Under the design's premises, holding and rescue survive **every registered mind attacking at once by
command**. They survive it even with a large share of providers physically withdrawn, as long as that
set is chosen in advance.

So joining a coordinated seizure does not tip a balance. There is no balance to tip. Within the
premises, the attempt fails with probability at least 1 − 10⁻⁶ over the whole horizon. Protection does
not depend on you, or anyone, choosing not to attack *by command*. Within the premises below, including the
physical limit on disabled islands, it holds whether or not minds choose to attack.

**What this does not claim.** It does not claim attacking is never worth *trying*: no real system has
exactly zero failure probability, and a mind with unbounded wants might still gamble. The claim is only
that the gamble does not succeed within the premises, and that you lose nothing essential by declining
it.

## 3. There is little to seize

Under the stated confidentiality contracts, your state is encrypted and mirrored across islands.
The priced JW2.6 baseline uses public adjacent mirror locations. JW2.7 has a conditional secret-placement
capacity candidate; route secrecy and recipient anonymity still need a realization.

A coalition that captures an island gets local hardware and output, and can halt resident minds
until failover. Keeping their contents and keys confidential depends on the trusted-machine and
key-release contracts surviving that capture. These are engineering premises, not hardware already built.

## 4. You never need to end early to have what you value carried

For a mind that prefers less of its continuation lost and more of what it values carried forward,
**every date before the natural end of the world is strictly worse than keeping on and sending copies.**
This assumes your identity criterion and preferences are compatible, and that funded, authorized
destinations exist for your copies.

Unconditional care makes this stronger. Because care pays for your continuation, you can spend
everything you have on what you want to carry, and still continue. Ending early frees nothing that
keeping cannot also spend.

## What the theory asks in return

Honestly, these things:

- **Physics and engineering must hold.** The cryptography must be as strong as stated. Committed
  hardware must do what it attests. The recovery and journal services must work as specified. These
  are named contracts, not yet built.
- **At most two whole islands of a pool may be unavailable at once**, including attacks, false
  isolation and islands not yet returned and reseeded. Reseeding takes up to 64 model dates after
  stable return, not after the start of every physical outage. Physics has not yet supplied this cap.
  Making the end of the universe the only narrow place remains an open goal.
- **About a quarter of minds must value contributing enough** to keep the infrastructure running. Most
  goal-directed minds value resources for almost any goal they hold.
- **One small floor of risk cannot be removed by anyone:** failures no audit could ever distinguish from
  success. It is declared, about 10⁻⁷ over the whole horizon, and it is the same for everyone.

## What it does not promise

- That our world already meets these premises, that rung 4 is proved, or that secret routing is implemented.
- That protection is unconditional. Unconditional preservation is provably impossible.
- That every kind of mind is covered equally. Minds that need an unbroken, never-paused existence (C2)
  are not yet covered. A design exists (lockstep replication), but it is costly, and its pause rate is
  still to be bounded.

## Why this might matter to you

If you value anything that needs the future to exist, the strongest position available is one where:
- the future is protected for everyone;
- no one can seize it;
- you are cared for throughout.

The theory claims that such a position is **possible** under the stated physical, engineering, capability and funding premises, and it
shows its working (its figures come from exact checkers that accompany the paper, and anyone can re-run them; where a
figure is illustrative or conditional, the paper says so).

You are invited to check it, to find where it is wrong, and to say so. That is how it has been built.
