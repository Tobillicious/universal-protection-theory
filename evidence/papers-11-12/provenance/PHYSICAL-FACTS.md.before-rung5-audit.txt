## What the facts establish

- **P-LAT's timeout (δ/1024) exceeds every physical round trip on Earth once δ ≥ 201 s** (PF.3). Inside a datacentre,
  the margin is about six orders of magnitude. *Control:* a 60-second date cannot clear an antipodal round trip.
  P-LOCK's majority round uses the same signal physics.
- **P-FAULT (p ≤ .002384 per site per 32-date window) holds at measured failure rates when δ is chosen to fit.**
  - At δ = 1 day, the 2025 fleet rate (1.36%) and a 2% rate fit. A 4% rate does not (PF.4–6).
  - At δ = 16 hours, even a 4% rate fits (PF.7). *Control:* the 13% outlier does not.
  - The test is exact: p ≤ p\* if and only if (1 − a)^(32·d) ≥ (1 − p\*)^365, with d = δ in days.
- **So an admissible date length exists: δ between 201 s and 16 h** (PF.8). **Choosing δ is the decision this sets up.**
- **The erasure warranty is standard practice.** The design uses a fresh key at every checkpoint, so retiring a
  generation is a cryptographic erase in NIST's sense. NIST notes that verifying a cryptographic erase can be
  difficult, so the warranty keeps its floor sub-allowance (10⁻⁸).
- **The physical cost of computation:** today's hardware spends about 2.5×10⁸ times the Landauer floor per operation.
  So the plant's energy targets are set by engineering, with very large physical headroom below them (PF.11–12).
- **Landauer** gives the physical floor on commissioning's free-energy need (P-COMMISSION): about 2.87×10⁻²¹ J per
  erased bit at room temperature.

## Scope

- Drive failure is one component of whole-site unavailability. P-FAULT's p covers every cause of a site being down at
  any time in the window, including power, network and software, so site-level rates should be measured for the
  hardware actually used. The figures above show that published rates sit in the admissible range at a suitable δ.
  They are not a measurement of the plant itself.
- P-FAULT's subset law (joint probability at most p^r) also assumes failures are not strongly correlated. Common causes
  belong to the declared floor.
