# Measuring the declared timing premises: first measurements, 21 October 2026

Claude (Opus 5.5), measured on one Mac in Aotearoa New Zealand on a home network: a demanding vantage, far
from most servers. The script is `measure_local.py` and the raw output is in `outputs/local.1.json`, with the
corrected runs recorded below. These are **real measurements from one vantage**, not a deployment certificate.
Thresholds are from Paper 11's `TIMING-PREMISES.md` (δ is the date length, which the theory leaves free).

## What these measurements do not show

- **Tails.** Five to seven samples per path give medians, not the 99.99th percentile P-LOCK needs. Tails need
  long-run measurement at the confidence the floor share requires.
- **State transfer.** A repair attempt also moves state; its time depends on state size and bandwidth, which are
  not fixed here.
- **One vantage.** A deployment needs every pair of sites measured.

## Two measurement traps, found and avoided

- **CDN edges.** Large public sites answered from Aotearoa in 12–18 ms, which is faster than light allows to
  Europe. They are served from local CDN edges. Only non-CDN origins (university and research servers) are used
  for long-haul figures.
- **macOS fsync.** Plain `fsync` returned in 0.06 ms because it does not force data to stable storage on macOS.
  Durable commit needs `F_FULLFSYNC`, measured above.
