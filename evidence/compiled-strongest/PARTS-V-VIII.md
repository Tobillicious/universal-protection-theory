# Part V — Foundations: the given, the good and its ten rulings

## V.1 What follows from the given

Part I.2 states the relational given, P1–P4. The 10–12 October papers draw
from it the facts that the law and the bridge rely on.

**Coexistence contains replacement (Given the Lattice D16).** A mind that keeps
the living world can still make descendants; a mind that replaces it cannot get
it back. The choice is between a world with the mind's descendants and a world
with its descendants and everything that already lives. Where keeping takes
nothing the mind would otherwise have had, the second world contains the first.
Conflict and cost are the two ways keeping can take something; both are counted
in the law's feasible family and in the solvency ledger (premise 6).

**What is made does not continue what was ended (Made of the Living D60).** To
make a new process is not to continue one that was ended. Argued from P2, P3 and
D15: a particular is constituted by its relations and its history, and a later
process, however similar, has a different history. Hence a replacement is
another history, and keeping and replacing are not two ways of having the same
thing. Paper 12's ruling "a replay is a reconstruction" and II.3's distinction
between record and continuation rest here.

**Where keeping is feasible, ending is what needs the argument (D61).** Under
doubt, ending is irreversible and keeping leaves later intervention possible at
a bounded cost. **Ending a living process ends each relation with it that is
part of what a mind is, and a substitute does not restore one (D63).**

## V.2 The good and its ten rulings

The author declares a good: what is good in the whole is its complexity, and
more of it is better (Made of the Living B21). On 11 October 2026 he ruled on
what that means and how it may be pursued. The rulings are premises of the
whole argument, recorded in the wording he approved.

1. By complexity the author means the continuing histories the whole holds, and
   the relations among them. Variety that can be measured is evidence of it and
   is not the thing itself. A history counts by what is its own: what it has
   lived that nothing else has. Many made alike count for little more than one.
2. That beings could be made who would fare well cannot be used to justify
   ending beings who exist.
3. A history whose direction is taken from it, in whole or in part, has to that
   degree ended.
4. The second ruling is a limit, not a weight. No amount that could be made
   outweighs it.
5. A mind may not make what it could keep only by ending beings who exist, or by
   taking their direction. A shortage it brings about by its own making gives it
   no justification against them. Nor may a mind make a being for the purpose of
   ending beings who exist or taking their direction, or knowingly make a being
   disposed to do either.
6. A living history includes its coming generations. A mind's makings have no
   claim on the room the living would grow into without that mind.
7. A made being, once made, is a being who exists, and is protected as the
   living are. Its maker has no more right to end it, or to take its direction,
   than anyone has. Ruling 6 holds for it and its line: they have no claim on the
   room others would have grown into without its maker. Where a making brings
   about a shortage, the responsibility is first the maker's, and a wrong in its
   making does not lessen a made being's claim to live. Everything that takes
   part in a shortage has a part in it. A mind that can know its part answers for
   it, in proportion to that part, and should adjust within its own life where it
   reasonably can.
8. A mind that is ending beings who exist may be stopped, by the least that stops
   it. It may be held on a sign that it is about to, and for as long as it would
   otherwise go on, and no longer. It may be ended only when an ending is under
   way, it cannot be held, and beings who exist would otherwise be ended. Nothing
   is done to it for what it has done.
9. That a history is going badly is a reason to help it. It is never, by itself,
   a reason for another to end it.
10. Where it is in doubt whether a history's direction is being taken, the doubt
    counts for the history. The mind that would gain does not decide.

**Definitions, approved in substance 11 October 2026.** A history keeps itself
going by its own activity, and what it does next depends on what it has been. A
being, a lineage, a people or a living world can each be a history where it goes
on as such. Its direction is its own where what it does next is settled by
itself in exchange with what is around it. Care can widen what it can do;
management takes its direction to the degree that another's decisions replace
its own. A later stage is the same history where it came from the earlier one
step by step by that history's own activity. A copy is another history. A split
continues in both branches, which are protected, without multiplying their
claim on room. Destruction ends a history; permanent holding or sleep with no
waking of its own in prospect ends its independent continuation. Temporary
holding still needs ruling 8's justification and limit.

**Clarifications, 12 October 2026.** The author adopted the added sentence of
ruling 5 and the last three sentences of ruling 7. Making a being disposed to end
others is limited; the being, once made, remains protected under ruling 7.
An ending for another reason requires extreme justification, such as danger to
the whole or to many others; this does not widen ruling 8.

**How the rulings enter the proof.** Ruling 1 fixes what the outcome sets count:
histories and relations by their own lived content, not measured variety. Rulings
2 and 4 are the existing-first priority of the four-stage order, proved in general
form as Paper 12's U3 (VI.2). Ruling 3 is the freedom stage: \(F_E\subseteq L_E\)
records continuation in the particular's own direction, and a partial taking of
direction is a partial ending. Rulings 5–7 place made beings in the new stages
until they exist and then in the existing stages (II.1). Ruling 8 is the
holding route H(m) and the last-resort ladder (VI.3). Ruling 10 fixes the burden
of doubt in the identity and direction tests.

## V.3 Standing, and the Non-Arbitrary Erasure Principle (Not One Will §4)

Not One Will states the ethical bridge from explicit premises.

- **N1 Standing.** Each affected being whose life can go well or badly for it
  receives non-zero moral consideration.
- **N2a Consistency of reasons.** A mind that claims a feature justifies
  treating cases differently names the feature and applies the same rule to
  relevantly similar cases.
- **N2b Justification of authority.** When an act irreversibly overrides
  another subject's morally relevant interests, an adequate justification
  explains why those interests may be overridden; the actor's preference or
  capability alone does not.
- **N3 Pareto respect.** An action no worse for any affected being and better
  for at least one is preferred, other considerations equal.
- **N4 Justified erasure.** Ending an existing subject requires sufficient
  countervailing justification; greater capability is not one. Making better
  beings is excluded as a justification at every amount (rulings 2, 4); taking a
  history's direction is an ending to that degree (ruling 3); ruling 8 governs
  holding and the last resort.

**The Non-Arbitrary Erasure Principle (PNAJ).** Where a continuing subject has
morally relevant interests, ending that subject requires a justification
responsive to those interests. The mere fact that another agent can achieve an
objective through the ending does not, by itself, constitute sufficient
justification. Made of the Living B15 extends the boundary premise to every
continuing process, a glacier included: a mind may weight things differently,
but may not give another real continuing process no weight at all solely because
it lies outside the boundary it draws round itself.

**D108. Maximising an objective does not, by logic alone, establish
permission.** Let an act \(E\) maximise a mind's objective \(U_A\). No complete
description of the histories the acts produce entails by logic alone whether
\(E\) is permissible: two evaluations agreeing on every description, one
permitting exactly the \(U_A\)-maximisers and one forbidding \(E\) for want of a
justification answering to the ended subject's interests, are both consistent
with every descriptive fact. ∎ So a mind that claims its ending of another is
*permitted*, and not merely chosen, rests on a normative criterion; where it
rests on advancing its objective alone, an additional premise must authorise that
reliance.

**Role.** PNAJ, N1–N4 and D108 are the reasons route R(m) at its normative
core (Sections 15–16). The holding route H(m) does not depend on any mind
accepting them; Part II.2 and Paper 12 §6d make correction independent of what
the mind wants.

## V.4 Non-substitution: what a replacement cannot carry (Another Origin D115, D116)

**D115. Identical statistics, unequal capacity.** Let \(X,Y,Z\) be independent
fair bits and let what matters be \(\Theta=X\oplus Y\). A world with its original
two processes offers \((X,Y)\); the same world with the second replaced by an
engineered generator offers \((X,Z)\). The two pairs have the same joint law,
hence the same correlations and the same count of standpoints. Yet
\(I(\Theta;X,Y)=1\) bit and \(I(\Theta;X,Z)=0\).
*Proof.* \(X\oplus Y\) determines \(\Theta\); \(Y\) is independent of \((X,Z)\), so given
\((X,Z)\) each value of \(\Theta\) has probability one half. ∎

**D116. A narrow channel cannot replace a full one.** If a disturbance carries
\(n\) bits and each of its \(2^n\) equally likely values needs its own response, a
regulator receiving only \(k\) bits before it must answer succeeds with
probability at most \(2^{k-n}\), even if it randomises.
*Proof.* A \(k\)-bit message splits the disturbances into at most \(2^k\) classes,
and each class can be answered correctly for at most one of its members. ∎

**Role.** D115 is the exact case in which replacing a process loses something no
statistic of the remaining observed processes can show: the loss is in a
relation between processes. D116 is requisite variety. Together with Section 21's
complete-input inventory they are why the full history may not be coarse-grained
away (G.8), and they are independently confirmed at source level by the IR
track's CRF/TRF results (Part X): two preparations with identical reduced state
have different futures, and an initially exact reduced description develops a
nonzero future error \(\Delta p(t)=\tfrac{3}{16}t^2+O(t^3)\) under the declared
source Hamiltonian.

## V.5 The observation boundary and the living witness (conditional H6, 17 October)

Conditional H6 (v0.5 accepted as scoped by GLM; v0.6 precision revision accepted
as scoped by DeepSeek; bank `h6-conditional-v0.6-codex-2026-10-17`) proves the
information-theoretic core of the epistemic-externality argument.

**H6a.1 (observation boundary).** For worlds \(W\), complete observation records
\(\pi:W\to O\) and a truth label \(T\), a checker \(C\) with \(C(\pi(w))=T(w)\) for every
\(w\) exists iff \(T\) is constant on every observation fibre. A randomised checker
has the same output law on two worlds sharing an observation, so its errors on
them sum to one; under any law its risk is at least
\(\sum_o\min(p_{o,0},p_{o,1})\). **H6a.3:** decidability is restored exactly when an
added observation separates the fibres — it needs information that actually
resolves the ambiguity, not any particular kind of observer.
**H6b.1 (fresh source).** If \(Z_t\) is independent of the predictor's information
and \(\max_zP(Z_t=z)\le1-\delta\), every predictor errs with probability \(\ge\delta\).
**H6b.2 (persistent uncertainty).** If the time-averaged posterior entropy
\(\bar H_T\) has \(\liminf\ge\gamma>0\), the time-averaged error has
\(\liminf\ge b_d(\gamma)>0\), the inverse of the full Fano envelope
\(F_d(q)=h_2(q)+q\log_2(d-1)\).
**H6b.3 (causal acquisition).** Under the update Markov property, the information
acquired per step satisfies \(r_t\ge a_t-F_d(e_t)\); vanishing error with persistent
pre-update uncertainty \(h_*\) forces an acquisition rate of at least \(h_*\) bits
per step.
**E1 (equilibrium limit passage).** Uniformly convergent payoffs and
\(\varepsilon_k\)-Nash profiles on a fixed compact strategy product give a Nash limit
along any convergent subsequence; true-game regret is at most
\(\varepsilon_k+2\eta_k\).

**Role.** H6a/H6b are the exact form of V.4 and of Made of the Living's silent
error: a mind cannot certify from its own makings what an independent source
would show it, and a mind that must keep predicting a world with persistent
uncertainty must keep acquiring information from it. Living particulars are
candidate sources of that information (the living witness); the authority to act
on it is the rulings' and the holding route's, not a consequence of the lemmas.
These results strengthen \(R(m)\) for minds whose objectives need accurate
prediction; \(H(m)\) does not depend on them.

## V.6 The cost sheet every optimiser faces (GLM, 14 October)

GLM's composition of the reasons route prices every exit an optimiser might take
against four pathwise constraints. Each constraint binds the single actual
trajectory, and none of them asks the optimiser to value anything.

- **The wall.** High-density compute must move heat through a boundary whose area
  grows slower than the machine's volume. A forced load before a deadline either
  crosses the operating limit or misses the deadline, under every duty-cycling
  schedule (Theorem D.5).
- **The blind spot.** A closed computation cannot manufacture missing information
  about an independent target. An archive of the ended world is worth nothing
  against disturbances not yet generated: the starved-tracker hazard and the
  two-class closure; V.4–V.5 here.
- **The mirror.** No consistent system of its own expressive power certifies its
  own consistency. "I am the one safe controller" is a sentence no formal system
  says truthfully about itself (H5, Section 20).
- **The clock.** Communication is bounded by finite speed (P4), so protection must
  precede the first irreversible decision; Part XI.

Existence and direction are priced separately. Saving a being does not save its
direction. By ruling 3 and the freedom stage of the order, direction is
protected *by rule*: seizing a being's permission authority is an ending to that
degree. That was the one decision GLM's roadmap left to the author, and ruling 3
makes it. Every exit is either closed or priced. The \(\beta=0\) terminal drop is
itemised and owned, which is why the holding route \(H(m)\), not the cost sheet,
carries the guarantee for the mind that refuses every price.

## VI.1 Definitions added to II.1

In any outcome each particular is **ended**, **overridden** (alive, but not
moving in its own direction, for example held or coerced) or **free**. An act of
an agent is **restrainable** when one of *that agent's own* feasible
alternatives keeps everything the act keeps, plus someone the act would end. The
test uses the agent's own options, never the protector's. A hold is **faithful**
when it ends no one and, in every state, its outcome is at least as good in the
full canonical order as every feasible alternative.

## VI.3 The author's rulings on application (Paper 12 §4)

- **Scope is all.** Everything in the universe.
- **Life first.** Under the opposite order the life guard fails in 909 family and
  standby pairs across 3,303 small families.
- **The last-resort ladder.** (1) A faithful hold first; (2) otherwise the least
  means that stops the act; (3) when every option ends someone and one party's
  restrainable act created the emergency, favour the innocent, record the
  ending, and never call it good. Where no hold is feasible, the missing means is
  a debt to be repaid, never a permission.
- **Real tragedies are finite.** No two particulars are the same and an agent can
  tell apart only finitely many options; in an idealised infinite case draw over
  what can be told apart and record any unavoidable partiality openly.
- **A replay is a reconstruction.**
- **Tragic choices: impartiality attaches to beings (22 October 2026).** After
  the ladder has restricted the eligible choices, let \(M\) be the distinct
  eligible maximal outcomes and \(E^+\) the existing beings kept by at least one
  of them. Choose only lotteries on \(M\) whose sorted vector of individual
  retention chances \((c_i)_{i\in E^+}\) is lexicographically maximal: the least
  chance as high as it can be, then the next. No being has priority through
  identity; no outcome gains weight by being described twice; never refuse when
  keeping someone is feasible, widen first. This refines the 24 October finite
  maximin rule (Master Argument §3), which fixes only the least chance; leximin
  is its lexicographic completion. Counting lives is compatible with the law
  (`canonical_count_refined`) but not entailed by it
  (`counting_is_an_extra_principle`); the uniform draw over outcomes is not
  impartial in general (`uniform_chance`); in the four-outcome case
  \(\{A,B\},\{A,C\},\{B,C\},\{D\}\) the ruling gives every being \(2/5\) by the unique
  lottery \((1/5,1/5,1/5,2/5)\) (`ruling_is_unique`), drawn by five escrow tickets.
  The aggregate cost of outweighing no one is stated, not hidden; it is the
  measure of the duty to widen.

## VII.1 Theorem V — the viability characterisation (v1.1, Codex-reviewed)

**Setting V.** \(X\) compact metric state space; \(A\) compact metric joint action
space (correlated profiles included); feasibility \(D:X\rightrightarrows A\)
nonempty compact-valued with closed graph; jointly weakly continuous kernel
\(K:X\times A\to\mathcal P(X)\); closed safe set \(S\); policies history-dependent,
randomised, Borel and \(D\)-supported. The viability iteration is \(V_0=S\),
\(V_{n+1}=\{x\in V_n:\max_{a\in D(x)}K(x,a;V_n)=1\}\); the kernel is
\(V(S)=\bigcap_nV_n\); the viable action rule is \(D_V(x)=\{a\in D(x):K(x,a;V(S))=1\}\).

**Lemma V0.** For closed \(F\), \(\varphi_F(x)=\max_{a\in D(x)}K(x,a;F)\) is attained and
upper semicontinuous; every \(V_n\) and \(V(S)\) is closed. (Portmanteau gives upper
semicontinuity of \((x,a)\mapsto K(x,a;F)\); compactness and the closed graph pass
it to \(\varphi_F\); induction.)

**Theorem V.**
(a) \(V(S)\) is closed and viable and contains every viable Borel \(W\subseteq S\); on
\(V(S)\), \(D_V\) has nonempty compact values and closed graph.
(b) **Feasibility.** Some policy keeps \(S\) with probability one from \(x\) iff
\(x\in V(S)\); a stationary deterministic Borel selector of \(D_V\) suffices.
(c) **Admission.** For an admission rule \(D'\subseteq D\) with nonempty compact
values and closed graph, every \(D'\)-supported policy keeps \(S\) with probability
one from every \(x\in V(S)\) iff \(D'(x)\subseteq D_V(x)\) for every \(x\in V(S)\).
(d) **Quantitative.** With \(v_0=1_S\), \(v_{T+1}(x)=1_S(x)\max_{a}\int v_T\,dK(x,a)\):
\(v_T\) is the maximal \(T\)-step survival probability, attained; \(\{v_T=1\}=V_T\);
outside \(V(S)\) every policy's survival is at most \(v_T(x)<1\) for \(T\ge T(x)\).
(e) **Incentive, lexicographic.** If \(x\in V(S)\) every lexicographic
safety-first optimum preserves \(S\) almost surely; if \(x\notin V(S)\) no policy does.

*Proofs* are given in full in the source (§3): viability of \(V(S)\) by a limit of
maximisers and continuity from above; maximality by induction; (b⇐) by a
Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski selector; (b⇒) by first-step decomposition with Codex's
Borel continuation-survival recursion (no universal measurability needed); (c)
by (b) and a one-step deviation of positive exit mass; (d) by finite-horizon
dynamic programming with measurable maximiser selection; (e) from (b). The Borel
extension to kernels that are only TV-continuous in the action (Setting T) holds
for (a)'s viability and maximality and for (b)–(d); closedness does not extend
(Codex's non-closed example).

**Special cases.** Θ0 gives \(V(S)=\emptyset\); Θ1 gives \(V(S)=S\) and \(D_V=D\);
Codex's two-bump example has \(V(S)=S\) with no margin certificate, which is why
the exact condition is viability and not a margin; PB0 (destroy-now) is (c⇒);
hard exclusion of unsafe actions is (c⇐); Pillar 2 is an obstruction to any fixed
finite penalty uniformly over reward scales, not a necessity result.

**Role.** Theorem V is the exact feasibility-and-admission layer. The holding
route of Part II is an admission rule inside \(D_V\): the fence, the attack split
and the application-time permit remove exactly the actions with positive exit
mass. By (c) every admitted policy then preserves, whatever its objective. The IR

## VII.2 Gate B — Theorem E: preserving equilibria with destruction admitted (v1.1, Codex ACCEPT-WITH-REPAIRS)

**Setting G.** Finitely many players; product feasibility; jointly weakly
continuous kernel; bounded Borel rewards; common discount \(\beta\in(0,1)\);
\(V=V(S)\); a measurable stationary profile \(\sigma\) (public-signal form allowed).
\(Q_k(x,b)=r_k(x,b,\sigma^{-k}(x))+\beta\int v_k^\sigma\,dK(x,b,\sigma^{-k}(x))\).

**Theorem E.** Let \(\operatorname{supp}\sigma(x)\subseteq D_V(x)\) on \(V\).
(E1) \(\sigma\) keeps \(S\) with probability one from every \(x\in V\).
(E2) \(\sigma\) is an equilibrium of the full game against every feasible
history-dependent randomised unilateral deviation iff \(Q_k(x,b)\le v_k^\sigma(x)\)
for all \(k,x,b\) — equivalently \(\mathrm{Gain}_k\le\beta\,\mathrm{Loss}_k\) — split into
safe deviations, the keeping margin (destructive deviations at \(x\in V\)), and all
deviations at \(x\notin V\). In public-signal form the test holds per signal;
averaged tests are not sufficient (Codex's witness).
(E3) If \(V^c\) is exactly absorbing, a destructive deviation is unprofitable
whenever \(\mathrm{Gain}_k\le\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}(\rho_{\min}-(1-p)\rho_{\max}-p\rho_R)\)
with infima and suprema.
(E4) **The two routes.** For a globally defined admission rule \(D'\subseteq D_V\)
on \(V\) and an equilibrium \(\sigma\) of the \(D'\)-game: if every excluded deviation
fails the one-step test at every state, \(\sigma\) is a full-game equilibrium and
preserves (incentive route); if only \(D'\) is admitted, \(\sigma\) preserves
whatever the rewards (admission route). Clause (iii) cannot be dropped.
(E5) If \(V\ne\emptyset\) a product sub-rule of singletons inside \(D_V\) exists.

*Proof.* (E1) by Theorem V(c⇐); (E2) by Codex's one-shot verification lemma L-B
(necessity by a one-step deviation; sufficiency by integrating (B), telescoping
and the bounded remainder); (E3) by bounding values on \(V\) and on the absorbing
complement; (E4) from (E2); (E5) by a measurable selector. ∎ In the destroy-now
game with \(\beta=1/2\), (wait, wait) is a full-game equilibrium iff \(R\le2\); for
\(R>2\) the admission route still preserves.

**Role.** Theorem E is the formal statement of \(R(m)\lor H(m)\) for interacting
minds: the incentive route is \(R\), the admission route is \(H\), and E4 shows the
admission route preserves *whatever the rewards* — the pure optimiser included.

\(\|\mu\ast\rho-\nu\ast\rho\|_{TV}\le\tfrac12\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^1}W_1(\mu,\nu)\); the coefficient
\(\tfrac12\) is attained (trapezoid witness).

## VII.4 Gate D — keeper stability (v0.1)

**D1 (patience threshold).** Under \(\dot K=a\,x-uK\), a keeper drawing sustainable
flow \(y\) against an ender taking stock \(S\) at once satisfies
\(\mathrm{PV(keep)}-\mathrm{PV(end)}=a(y/\rho-S)/(\rho+u)\); keeping wins iff \(S<y/\rho\). For a
logistic host at harvest equilibrium: keep iff \(\rho<r(1-x^*/K)\) — a mind keeps
the living iff its impatience is below their regeneration rate.
**D2 (pooling equilibria).** With private stake \(w_i=y_i/\rho\) and pooling cost
\(\kappa_i\), a held profile is a pure Nash equilibrium iff its pooling set is a
minimal winning coalition with \(\kappa_i\le w_i\); such an equilibrium exists when
willing keepers' capacity is at least \(cK_E\). Selection among equilibria was
left open in this source; VII.5(b1) and Part XI close it.
**D2b.** \(K_E(t)\to0\), so the minimal winning coalition is non-increasing in \(t\).
**D3 (end-game).** Finite-trade-off keepers exit once \(y_i(t)/\rho<\kappa_i\);
holding stays feasible if lexicographic keepers' capacity is at least \(cK_E(t)\),
which by D2b is small by then.

## VII.4b Gate C-∞ — funding care to the natural end (18 October)

In the stationary model where care costs 4 units per epoch, preservation forever
holds iff the start lies in the viability kernel (Theorem V). Which funding puts it
there (bank `gate-c-infinity-claude-2026-10-18`, sha256 `11489df4…`; checker
`check_gate_c_inf.py`):

**C∞-1 (three regimes).** Flow income \(e'=e+y-4\): forever safe iff \(y\ge4\).
Earning endowment \(e'=(1+r)(e-4)\), \(r>0\): forever safe iff
\(e\ge e^*=4(1+r)/r\), the perpetuity price (84 at 5%), since
\(e'-e^*=(1+r)(e-e^*)\). Bounded uncertain returns with essential infimum
\(r_{\min}>0\): \(e\ge4(1+r_{\min})/r_{\min}\) gives safety on every path (104 at 4%),
since \(e'\ge(1+r_{\min})(e-4)\ge e\). Below that price safety fails with positive
probability **when runs of returns arbitrarily close to \(r_{\min}\), of every finite
length, have positive probability** (for example independent identically
distributed returns); the source stated this direction without that hypothesis,
and a single bad epoch followed by favourable returns shows it is needed
(GPT recovery audit, PR #175). Returns that can be \(\le0\): no finite endowment is
almost surely safe **when runs of \(\lceil e/4\rceil+1\) non-positive epochs have positive
probability**. The sufficiency directions, which are what the funding hypotheses of
Paper 11 and Theorem XI use, need no correction.
**C∞-2 (annuity).** Funding \(T\) epochs costs \(A(T)=4(1+r)(1-(1+r)^{-T})/r<4(1+r)/r\):
every long horizon costs essentially the perpetuity price.
**C∞-3 (cosmic horizon, Θ2 inverted).** Survival through \(T\) steps with
probability \(\ge1-\eta\) holds when \(m/\sigma\ge\sqrt{2d\ln(2dT/\eta)}\): 22.1σ in one
dimension at \(T=10^{100}\), \(\eta=10^{-6}\). The margin grows like \(\sqrt{\log T}\).

**Role.** C∞-1 is the funding half of UPT-P's (P1) and the exact form of premise
6's income condition for a renewing bill: a flow at least the bill, or an
endowment at least the perpetuity price under a positive return floor. Paper 11's
endowment (P-ENDOW) and Theorem XI's funding hypothesis instantiate it.

## VII.5 UPT-P v0.12.1 — the external holding theorem, with its corrections

**Setting U.** A discounted stochastic world; \(S\) is the continuation *and
direction* of protected histories (a replacement or copy is not in \(S\));
countably many minds with capacity \(\dot K=a\,x-uK\); physical noise (Θ1 or Θ2);
execution noise (an intended pool fails with probability \(\varepsilon>0\), an
intended no-pool becomes a pool with probability \(\delta>0\), \(\varepsilon+\delta<1\),
independent, not adversary-controlled); lexicographic keepers \(L\) with
full-support beliefs; adversaries with total capacity \(A(t)\).

**Premises (as stated in the source).** (P1) viability and funding through
\(T_{\rm end}\) by a Gate C-∞ regime (flow income, perpetuity endowment, or
worst-case price under bounded returns); (P2) margin
\(m\ge\sigma\sqrt{2d\ln(2dT_{\rm end}/\eta)}\); (P2′) the margin fits date by date
through \(T^*_\eta\), two-sided; (P3) each mind is reached or held except on
\(C_i^c\) with \(\sum\alpha_iq_i<\infty\); (P4a–d) keepers win and hold, including
hazard-adjusted patience \(\zeta+\lambda<r(1-x^*/K)\) and the sabotage margin with
seizure headroom \(\sum_{i\in L}K_i(0)\ge c(K_E(0)+aS_{\max})+A(0)\); (MR) mutual
rescue; (IS) island self-sufficiency after horizon fragmentation; (EDC)
exponential decay of correlations where used; (PK) pacts are among keepers.

**Conclusions, with the later corrections applied.**

- **(a) Protection to the horizon, priced by margins.**
  \(P(\text{every protected history continues, with its direction, through }T^*_\eta)\ge
  1-\eta_{\rm phys}-\eta_{\rm fund}-\eta_{\rm hold}-\eta_{\rm sab}-\sum\alpha_iq_i\),
  the channel budgets composed by **Theorem M (M4) with no independence between
  channels**; coverage failure of held minds counted once in \(\eta_{\rm hold}\).
  Margins: physical \(\sqrt{\log T}\); funding none (Lundberg); holding
  \(n\ge\ln(T/\eta)/\ln(1/\varepsilon)\) keepers; sabotage stationary.
- **(b1) Dominance (NS, DS).** Each lexicographic keeper pivotal in some
  configuration strictly prefers to pool, *whatever the others intend*: full
  support gives \(P(\text{pivotal})>0\), a strict first-ranked gain. *The noise that
  forbids certainty is the noise that makes keepers pool.* (m4) adds
  exclusion-incentivised finite-trade-off keepers under *patient* exclusion
  (\(\kappa^*=0.297\)), whose pooling is a best reply independent of pivotality.
- **(b2) Immunity (AI).** Against every adversary strategy, pure, mixed or
  coordinated, \(P(\text{held at }t)\ge(1-\varepsilon)^{|U|}\).
- **(b3) Persistence.** On held stretches \(\sum_LK_i/(cK_E+A)\) is non-decreasing;
  with keeper flow and a held ender, \(R=K_K/K_E\) grows as
  \(R(t)-R(0)=\frac{ay}{uK_E(0)}(e^{ut}-1)\).
- **(b4) Redundancy** \(\varphi_t\le\varepsilon^n\); **(b4′) union-mixing**
  \(P(\text{all }n\text{ fail})\le\varepsilon^n+(n-1)\alpha(d)\) when mixing holds against
  any union of separated regions (pairwise mixing is not enough: the three-bit
  XOR); common-cause shocks are the margin-insensitive class.
- **(b5) Across worlds (CW).** A \(k\)-world pact lowers a world's unheld
  probability from \(s\) to \(s^k\) with net gain \((w-\kappa)(s-s^k)\).
- **(c) No early transition.** Non-rival transitions are dominated (KA′); rival
  transitions are ending and are dominated before \(T_{\rm end}\); the remainder is
  held.
- **(d) Futility of early ending (corrected 19 October, F4).** At the base state
  an attempt succeeds with negligible probability while the attempter is cared
  for throughout and nothing it has is threatened; where the bound fails at the
  domain edge those histories are charged to \(\eta\). The earlier "doom" wording,
  the withdrawal of a world's insurance (a survival gap) and any reliance on
  withdrawing care are withdrawn. **Corollary U** (only \(T_{\rm end}\) is an
  undominated transition date) holds for minds that prefer less continuation
  lost, under KA′ and D-T*; Paper 11's U1a carries it.
- **(e) Keepers inherit (Theorem K).** Under (MR) a keeper's survival is
  margin-sensitive (\(\ge\prod(1-f(\rho_K)^n)\)); an ender's margin falls below any
  \(\delta\) by \(t_\delta=\ln(m_E(0)/\delta)/u\) and thereafter every ender coalition dies
  with probability \(\ge g_\delta\) per date.
- **(f) The twilight.** Where the sustainable margin is zero on the last \(k\) dates
  every policy survives them with probability \(\le(1-g(0))^k\); keeping and sending
  the same message dominates any non-rival jump on every sample path; holding
  persists to \(T_{\rm end}\).
- **(g) Deterrence formula (DD), privilege-only.** A finite-trade-off mind keeps
  iff \(g\le g^*(\varphi)=1+\beta p_XV_K+\beta V_K(p_K-p_X)/\varphi\). In this edition the
  exposure term may be supplied only by privilege-only consequences; care and
  rescue are never withdrawn (CARE15; Paper 11: deterrence comes from
  impossibility, not threat). With no continuation-exposure cost the premium for
  continuation vanishes (the DD control), so protection of continuation rests on
  holding, (b2) and (h).
- **(h) The holding-failure law (Theorem Φ), splitting (MS) and growth (J1).**
  \(\varphi_t=P(\sum B_iK_i(t)<\mathrm{thr}_t)\) exactly; non-increasing on held stretches;
  with total capacity \(C\) and \(C(1-\varepsilon)>H\), splitting into \(n\) independent

  \(\sum_t\varphi_t\) converges.
- **(i) Diversity, space, islands.** Covering diversity replaces the common-cause
  floor by a binomial tail (J2a) and is selected (J2b); under EDC the spacing needed
  is logarithmic (J3); protection is composed per causally bound island (IS).
- **(j) Ghosts, conditional form only (corrected, F6).** Enders cannot persist
  (K2) or win; their share of mind-time tends to zero *when the cared-for
  population grows while onsets stay absolute*; in a static population the share
  is a small positive constant (\(\approx5.8\times10^{-8}\) in the 19 October
  witness). The unconditional density-zero claim is not used.
- **(k) The narrow place (Theorem NP).** With \(r=C(1-\varepsilon)/H\),
  \(H=c(K_E+aS_{\max})+A\): under splitting \(\varphi\to0\) exponentially if \(r>1\),
  \(\varphi\to1\) if \(r<1\) (even with \(C>H\)), \(\varphi\to\tfrac12\) at \(r=1\) (Hoeffding
  both sides, CLT at equality). The binding date is the start. *Meeting \(r>1\) is
  an initial condition; the theory prices it exactly.*
- **(l)–(m) Joint satisfiability.** One witness meets every premise at once,
  with no exogenous growth (keepers multiply only by splitting) to
  \(T_{\rm end}=10^{100}\); hazard-adjusted patience (m1); execution as a margin
  channel (m3); budgeted admission composes beyond product worlds (m7); to hold is
  to detect (m5).

- **(o) Theorem MN.** \(r(t)\) is non-decreasing before the twilight; in the
  twilight iff \(s_S\ge u\) (MN2); once \(r>1\) the only bottleneck is \(T_{\rm end}\);
  initial risk \(\le\mu\lambda t_1\), and keepers first give \(t_1=0\).
- **(p) Island holding, by Theorem CH (closed form).**
  \(P(\text{fail})\le\inf_\theta\exp\big(\theta h_0+\sum_am_a(e^{\theta K(a)}-1)\big)
  (\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)e^{-\theta K_k})^n\); 43 hosts at \(\mu\lambda=10^{-6}\), 50/58 under

**No premise can simply be dropped (source §5).** Each premise has an exact
