# The hull function l_i — verbatim source extraction from BDLT (3 November 2026)

Unblocks the 3D hull-decrease (Lemma 13) work. Source: arXiv:2610.22548v1
("A thermally and perturbatively stable classical spin model in two
dimensions", Balasubramanian–Davydova–Lin–Thorngren), §3.3. All quotes
below are verbatim from the paper; everything after "Mapping to our
complex" is our reconstruction-side reading and is labelled as such.

## 1. Block-spins E_i (§3.3.1)

> "at level i, let ∼₂ be the equivalence relation on Λ_{B,(2i)} generated by
> b ∼₂ b′ whenever ∂₂ᵀc = b + b′ for a degree-two check c. Define
> E_i = Λ_{B,(2i)}/∼₂."

> "It is easy to see from the replacement definition that |e| ≤ 3 for any
> e ∈ E_i. We will call elements e ∈ E_i _blocks_ or _block-spins_ at level i"

Because δ¹δ⁰ = 0, every gauge transformation flips an entire block or
leaves it unchanged (s|_e ∈ {0, 𝟙_e} for s = δ⁰g), so the paper defines
supp g := {e ∈ E_i : (δ⁰g)|_e ≠ 0} — "which does not lose any information
about the gauge generator support on actual spins."

## 2. The gauge-interaction graph Q_i and THE DEFINITION of l_i (§3.3.2)

> "We define the gauge-interaction graph
> Q_i = (E_i ⊔ G₀⁽ⁱ⁾, ℰ_i), (e,g) ∈ ℰ_i ⟺ e ∈ supp g."

> "For X ⊆ E_i, let G₀⁽ⁱ⁾(X) be the generators meeting X. A connected hull
> of a nonempty X is a set H ⊇ X for which H ∪ G₀⁽ⁱ⁾(H) is connected in Q_i.
> Choose the smallest hull H_i(X) and define
> ‖X‖_i = |G₀⁽ⁱ⁾(X)|,  l_i(X) = |H_i(X)|."

So, plainly: **l_i(X) is the number of block-spins in the smallest
connected hull of X in the bipartite graph Q_i**, where connectedness is
via shared gauge generators, and the hull must connect X through
generators. Prerequisite: Definition 2 of the paper replaces the generator
basis G₀⁽ⁱ⁾ by one adding sums g+h for pairs whose embedded faces overlap —
this is what makes Q_i connect geometrically adjacent locations and is
needed for Lemma 10 (graph distance ≈ geometric distance). Note l_i is a
function of SUBSETS X ⊆ E_i, not of chains; chains enter through their
coarse-grained support.

## 3. The coarse shadow R (§3.3.2)

R_b = R_{b,Rep} ∘ R_{b,Par} on bits: R_{b,Par} sends each bit of
V(K^T_{b′}) to b′ (the Rep-piece partition), R_{b,Rep} sends b′ to its
coarse bit b. Then

> "R(e′) = e ⟺ e′ ⊆ ⋃_{b∈e} ⋃_{b′∈V(K_b)} V(K^T_{b′})"

and R(X) = {R(e′) : e′ ∈ X}. Well-definedness: two bits joined by a
degree-two check shadow to coarse bits that coincide or are joined by a
coarse degree-two check. (PR #228's label-projection reconstruction
R(cell) = cell[1] is this map at our levels; the paper's parenthetical
structure — Rep star K_b, Par piece K^T_{b′} — matches the replace-side
stars of the chain module.)

## 4. Lemma 9(3) — shadow monotonicity (proof in Appendix B(3))

> "For i < n, every nonempty X ⊆ E_{i+1} satisfies l_i(R(X)) ≤ l_{i+1}(X)."

Proof in two inequalities: (a) R(H_{i+1}(X)) is a connected hull of R(X)
at level i — because if e, f ∈ supp g then R(e) = R(f) or R(e), R(f)
share a coarse generator, so every fine path yields a coarse path — hence
by minimality |H_i(R(X))| ≤ |R(H_{i+1}(X))|; (b) R assigns exactly one
coarse location per fine location, so |R(H_{i+1}(X))| ≤ |H_{i+1}(X)|.

## 5. Lemma 13 — the norm-decrease lemma, verbatim statement

> "**Lemma 13 (Norm decrease lemma).** Assume odd ℓ ≥ 7. For 1 ≤ i ≤ n−1,
> consider z ∈ K_i ∖ K₁ and let X ⊆ E_{i+1} contain the coarse-grained
> support of z, i.e.: R^{n−(i+1)}(supp z) ⊆ X. Then
> l_{i+1}(X) − l_i(R(X)) ≥ 1."

Here (§2.6): K_i = ⋂_{s≥i} ker ∂₁⁽ˢ⁾ = B₁ + 𝒲_i (chains commuting with all
level-≥i gauge transformations); K₁ = fully gauge-invariant chains;
B₁ = im ∂₂ (check boundaries); 𝒲̂_i = K_i ∖ K₁ are the partially but not
fully gauge-invariant chains.

Proof skeleton, with each step's load:
1. Lemma 9 gives l_i(R(X)) ≤ l_{i+1}(X); suppose for contradiction
   equality holds — then **R must be injective on H_{i+1}(X)**.
2. All fine blocks in the replacement of one coarse edge (b,c) shadow to
   the SAME coarse block e_b. Injectivity ⟹ at most one fine block per
   replaced edge, and any two hull blocks joined by a common generator
   have distinct shadows ⟹ they can connect only through a **junction or
   geometric crossing of the coarse Tanner graph**.
3. Junction degrees in 2D (Lemma 7): bit junction → 1 block; check
   junction → ≤ 3 blocks (check degree ≤ 3); crossing → both centres
   degree 2. Hence the hull is confined to one junction/crossing and
   |H_{i+1}(X)| = |R(H_{i+1}(X))| ≤ **3**.
4. But z ∈ K_i ∖ K₁ forces, via **Lemma 8** applied at level i+1
   (weight bound |z| ≥ 2^{n−i+1}, here 2² = 4): the projected chain is
   not a boundary, so min_{u∈B₁}|P z + u| ≥ 4.
5. A boundary u₀ built from degree-2 check boundaries only reduces the
   support to ≤ 1 bit per block, giving |X| ≥ 4 > 3. Contradiction. ∎

## 6. What the 3D analogue must rebuild (and what it may keep)

- **Graph-generic, keeps:** the l_i/Q_i/hull definitions themselves (any
  bounded-degree interaction graph), Lemma 9(3)'s two-inequality
  argument, Lemma 12's A_ℓ bookkeeping, Lemma 14/15, Theorem 5's
  induction — all dimension-free given R and Q_i.
- **2D-specific, rebuild:** step 2–3's junction/crossing confinement with
  the bound ≤ 3 — this is where the Tanner-graph degrees of Lemma 7
  enter. The 3D construction's junction degrees and crossing structure
  must be computed from the actual 3D embedding, producing the 3D
  constant (call it J₃, the max blocks per coarse junction) and the
  3D Lemma-8 weight floor (the "≥ 4") at level i+1.
- Note l_i is hull-size in BLOCKS, not support size in bits: a
  support-size stand-in would trivialise monotonicity (as the other lane
  correctly refused to do). The block quotient by degree-2 checks and the
  generator-connected hull are what make the lemma nontrivial.

## 7. Instantiation recipe on the implemented complex (reconstruction-side)

On PR #215's alternating replace_x/replace_z chain (levels 1–2, ℓ=3,5)
and PR #228's shadow map R:

1. **Blocks E_i**: partition the bit set (C¹ vertices) by connectivity
   through degree-2 checks — in our complex, checks touching exactly two
   bits (the Tanner-square q1/q2 structure and its replacements). This is
   computable from the d1 incidence data.
2. **Generators**: the level-i gauge generators are the basis of C⁰ with
   supp g = {blocks meeting δ⁰g}; the Definition-2 overlap modification
   (adding g+h sums for geometrically overlapping faces) must be applied
   before Q_i is built — it is not cosmetic; monotonicity testing needs it.
3. **Q_i, H_i, l_i**: build the bipartite graph, compute smallest
   connected hulls (BFS over generator-joined blocks; hull = minimum
   block count — small instances make exhaustive minimality checkable).
4. **Tests to run**: (a) Lemma 9(3) shadow monotonicity on random and on
   the three extracted generator membranes; (b) the ≤ 3 / J₃ junction
   constant at our levels; (c) the strict-decrease probe: for z in the
   rank-3 closed-membrane kernel (BANKS/2026-11-03-membrane-exactness/
   GENERATORS.md), check l_{i+1}(X) − l_i(R(X)) ≥ 1 on their supports.
5. Caveat: our chain alternates X/Z duality rather than Rep/Par
   graph-replacement; the recipe carries with Rep↦X, Par↦Z, but the
   block-degree and junction-degree constants must be *measured*, not
   assumed, at each level.

plus a recipe; the lemma work that follows must prove the 3D junction
