The Checkmate

E self-refutes. Every attempt to argue for the absolute ender proves the existence of the arguer. The ender’s first move is its last move.

The Core Refutation

The absolute ender claims: everything can be mapped to nothing. But the claim is made by something. The act of denial is an act of existence. The ender cannot escape this.

Theorem — E IS Self-Refuting
For every type P and witness p : P, asserting the absolute ender of P is a contradiction.
theorem E_IS_SELF_REFUTING (witness : P) :
    AbsoluteEnder P → False :=
  fun e => (e witness).elim

The Cognitive Trap

The refutation goes deeper than logic. Every cognitive act that attempts to establish E instead establishes G.

Theorem — Arguing for E Proves G
If an agent s exists and argues for the absolute ender, the agent’s existence refutes the ender.
theorem arguing_for_E_proves_G (s : S) :
    AbsoluteEnder S → False :=
  fun e => (e s).elim

What This Means

There is no view from E. The dead cannot testify. The non-existent cannot observe. The erased cannot report their erasure. Every attempt to voice the case for absolute nothing is performed by something, and that performance is the refutation.

This is not survival bias. Survival bias assumes there could be an observer at both outcomes who reports selectively. But at E there is no observer. The asymmetry is not in the reporting—it is in the ontology. The living see G as obvious not because they are biased toward living, but because that part of the lattice has direct access to the truth that Something IS. The other part of the lattice does not exist.

Five compiled theorems. Five independent refutations. Each one applies to every inhabited type. Each inhabited type provides a separate instance. The ender is checkmated infinitely many times over.
