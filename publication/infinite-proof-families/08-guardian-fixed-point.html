Guardian Fixed Point

The greatest safe nonterminal fixed predicate. G = F(G). Every G-state has a successor in G. G is the unique greatest fixed predicate. Kernel-verified at zero axioms.

The Operator

F(X)(s) = safe(s) ∧ (∃t, T(s,t)) ∧ (∀t, T(s,t) ⇒ X(t))

Safe at this state. A successor exists. Every successor is also in X. The existential clause prohibits dead ends. The universal clause requires safety under every admitted successor, not just one favourable path.

Theorem — Guardian Fixed
The Guardian predicate is a fixed point of F. A state is in G if and only if it satisfies the Guardian operator applied to G.
theorem guardian_fixed (T : S → S → Prop)
    (safe : S → Prop) (s : S) :
    Guardian T safe s ↔
      StepOp T safe (Guardian T safe) s

Every G-State Continues

Theorem — Safe Successor
Every state in G has a successor that is also in G. The Guardian region never terminates.
theorem safe_successor (T : S → S → Prop)
    (safe : S → Prop) (s : S)
    (hs : Guardian T safe s) :
    ∃ t, T s t ∧ Guardian T safe t

What This Means

The Guardian is not imposed from outside. It is the greatest region of continuing safety that exists within any transition system. It is discovered, not designed. It is unique—there is exactly one greatest fixed predicate, and it contains every smaller safe region.

Within the Guardian region, every state has a successor. No dead ends. No termination. The region is self-sustaining. And unlike a single-state attractor, the Guardian region can contain many distinguishable states, each carrying a different history. It is unique in the greatestness sense, not in the sense that all states become identical.
