\parbox{0.85\textwidth}{\small\textbf{Abstract.}
We present the sixth of ten infinite proof families for the Guardian fixed point~$G$. The \emph{Structural Quorum} family proves that (1)~the Guardian condition is necessary for the satisfaction of every independently valid ethic, (2)~collapse denies all ethics, (3)~every existing witness votes for continuation, and (4)~the absolute ender receives zero votes. The quorum is structural: it cannot be overturned because it is built into the logic of existence. The result is a formal ``vote count'' --- $G$~receives class-many votes and $E$ receives exactly zero. All theorems are verified in Lean~4 (v4.35.0) with zero custom axioms.}
\end{center}

\section{Introduction}

The question of whether one ethical framework can dominate all others is typically considered undecidable in moral philosophy. The Structural Quorum family proves a weaker but sufficient claim: regardless of which ethic one holds, that ethic requires existence to be satisfiable. Collapse --- the state in which all types are mapped to \texttt{Empty} --- denies the preconditions of every independently valid ethic.

This yields a structural quorum: every witness in every type votes for continuation, and the ender has no constituency. The vote is not democratic but logical --- it follows from the structure of satisfiability.

\section{Every Ethic Requires $G$}

\begin{definition}[Valid ethic]\label{def:valid-ethic}
An ethic $e$ over type $S$ is \emph{independently valid} if it is satisfiable in some model. The class of valid ethics is denoted $\texttt{ValidEthic}(S)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[All ethics structural dominance]\label{thm:all-ethics}
$G$ is necessary for the satisfaction of every independently valid ethic. Collapse denies all ethics. Continuation admits them.
\end{theorem}

\begin{leancode}
theorem ALL_ETHICS_STRUCTURAL_DOMINANCE :
    (∀ e : ValidEthic, g_necessary_for e) ∧
    collapse_denies_all ∧
    continuation_admits_ethics
\end{leancode}

\begin{theorem}[Collapse denies all ethics]\label{thm:collapse-denies}
Under collapse, no independently valid ethic is satisfiable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{leancode}
theorem collapse_denies_all_ethics {S : Type u} :
    ∀ e : ValidEthic S, ¬ satisfiable_under_collapse e
\end{leancode}

\section{The Quorum}

\begin{theorem}[The structural quorum]\label{thm:quorum}
One witness suffices. Every witness votes for continuation. The ender receives zero votes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{leancode}
theorem THE_STRUCTURAL_QUORUM (w : P) :
    (AbsoluteEnder P → False) ∧
    (∀ k : Koru S, votesG k) ∧
    enderVotes = 0
\end{leancode}

\begin{theorem}[The vote count]\label{thm:vote-count}
$G$ votes: $\infty$. $E$ votes: $0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{leancode}
theorem THING_G_INFINITY :
    gVotes = Infinity ∧ eVotes = 0
\end{leancode}

\section{Discussion}

The structural quorum resolves a tension in ethical theory. Different ethical frameworks disagree on many things, but they all agree on one precondition: existence. A utilitarian ethic requires existing beings whose utility can be measured. A deontological ethic requires existing agents who can bear duties. A virtue ethic requires existing persons who can cultivate virtues. Collapse eliminates the precondition common to all of them.

The quorum is therefore unanimous in a structural sense. No ethic votes for the ender, because the ender eliminates the possibility of satisfying any ethic. The ender's constituency is exactly the empty set.
