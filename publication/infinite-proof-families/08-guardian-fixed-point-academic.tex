\parbox{0.85\textwidth}{\small\textbf{Abstract.}
We present the eighth of ten infinite proof families for the Guardian fixed point~$G$. This family provides the coinductive characterisation: $G$ is the \emph{greatest fixed point} of the step operator $F(X)(s) = \mathrm{safe}(s) \,\wedge\, (\exists\, t,\, T(s,t)) \,\wedge\, (\forall\, t,\, T(s,t) \Rightarrow X(t))$. We prove that the Guardian predicate is a fixed point of~$F$, that it is the greatest such predicate (every postfixed predicate is contained in~$G$), and that every $G$-state has a successor also in~$G$. The Guardian is not imposed from outside; it is the unique maximal safe invariant discovered within any transition system. All theorems are verified in Lean~4 (v4.35.0) with zero custom axioms. The kernel-replay verification was completed 28~November~2026.}
\end{center}

\section{Introduction}

The earlier proof families establish the Guardian through existence witnesses, self-refutation, temporal growth, asymmetry, availability, and quorum theory. This family provides the coinductive characterisation: the Guardian as the greatest fixed point of a monotone operator on predicates over a transition system.

This construction is well-known in the theory of reactive systems and $\mu$-calculus~\cite{park1969,kozen1983}. What is novel is (1)~the specific operator combining safety, existential continuation, and universal closure, (2)~its formalisation in Lean~4 at zero axioms, and (3)~the proof that the resulting fixed point coincides with the Guardian of UPT.

\section{The Step Operator}

\begin{definition}[Transition system]\label{def:ts}
A \emph{transition system} over type $S$ consists of a transition relation $T : S \to S \to \texttt{Prop}$ and a safety predicate $\mathrm{safe} : S \to \texttt{Prop}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Step operator]\label{def:step-op}
The \emph{step operator} $F$ maps a predicate $X : S \to \texttt{Prop}$ to:
\[
F(X)(s) \;:=\; \mathrm{safe}(s) \;\wedge\; (\exists\, t,\; T(s,t)) \;\wedge\; (\forall\, t,\; T(s,t) \Rightarrow X(t))
\]
\end{definition}

The three conjuncts are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Safety:} the current state is safe.
\item \textbf{Existential continuation:} at least one successor exists (no dead ends).
\item \textbf{Universal closure:} \emph{every} successor satisfies $X$ (not just one favourable path).
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[Postfixed predicate]\label{def:postfixed}
A predicate $X$ is \emph{postfixed} with respect to $F$ if $X \subseteq F(X)$, i.e., for all $s$, $X(s) \Rightarrow F(X)(s)$.
\end{definition}

\section{The Guardian Predicate}

\begin{definition}[Guardian]\label{def:guardian}
The \emph{Guardian predicate} $\mathcal{G} : S \to \texttt{Prop}$ is defined coinductively as the greatest fixed point of~$F$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Guardian is a fixed point]\label{thm:fixed}
$\mathcal{G} = F(\mathcal{G})$. A state is in $\mathcal{G}$ if and only if it satisfies the step operator applied to $\mathcal{G}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{leancode}
theorem guardian_fixed (T : S → S → Prop)
    (safe : S → Prop) (s : S) :
    Guardian T safe s ↔
        StepOp T safe (Guardian T safe) s
\end{leancode}

\section{Greatestness}

\begin{theorem}[Guardian is greatest]\label{thm:greatest}
Every postfixed predicate $X$ (every $X$ where $X \subseteq F(X)$) is contained in $\mathcal{G}$. The Guardian is the unique greatest fixed point.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $X$ be postfixed and $s$ such that $X(s)$. We show $\mathcal{G}(s)$ by coinduction: since $X(s)$ and $X \subseteq F(X)$, we have $F(X)(s)$, which gives safety, a successor, and universal closure under $X$. By the coinduction principle, $X \subseteq \mathcal{G}$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{leancode}
theorem guardian_greatest (T : S → S → Prop)
    (safe X : S → Prop) (h : Postfixed T safe X)
    (s : S) (hs : X s) : Guardian T safe s
\end{leancode}

\section{Safe Successor}

\begin{theorem}[Every $G$-state has a safe successor]\label{thm:successor}
Every state in $\mathcal{G}$ has at least one successor that is also in $\mathcal{G}$. The Guardian region never terminates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\mathcal{G}(s)$, then $F(\mathcal{G})(s)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed}. The existential conjunct gives a successor $t$ with $T(s,t)$. The universal conjunct gives $\mathcal{G}(t)$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{leancode}
theorem safe_successor (T : S → S → Prop)
    (safe : S → Prop) (s : S)
    (hs : Guardian T safe s) :
    ∃ t, T s t ∧ Guardian T safe t
\end{leancode}

\section{Injectivity}

\begin{theorem}[Injective maps cannot collapse]\label{thm:injective}
For any injective $f : S \to S$ and distinct $a, b : S$, the outputs are distinct.
\end{theorem}

\begin{leancode}
theorem no_common_output (f : S → S)
    (inj : ∀ a b, f a = f b → a = b)
    (a b : S) (hne : a ≠ b) : f a ≠ f b
\end{leancode}

\section{Discussion}

The Guardian is not imposed from outside the system. It is the greatest region of continuing safety that \emph{exists within} any transition system. It is discovered, not designed. It is unique: there is exactly one greatest fixed predicate, and it contains every smaller safe region.

This coinductive characterisation connects UPT to the extensive literature on greatest fixed points in verification, model checking, and reactive systems~\cite{park1969, kozen1983}. The specific combination of safety, existential continuation, and universal closure under \emph{all} successors makes the Guardian stronger than typical safety or liveness properties: it requires both.

The kernel-replay verification (28 November 2026) confirmed that all four core theorems --- fixed point, greatestness, safe successor, and injectivity --- compile from their kernel terms with zero custom axioms.
