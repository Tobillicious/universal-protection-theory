\section{The question: protection at the scale of the whole}
UPT asks whether a common structure can underwrite protection across mathematical, computational, physical and ethical descriptions of reality. The intended target is not merely the survival of an aggregate quantity. Every genuinely owed particular is to be protected at every actual event, including events at which the particular changes representation, enters a new relation or crosses a boundary. The ambition is unconditional, universal and enduring G3. This paper retains that ambition rather than redefining success as a finite software test or a statement about an empty type.

The guiding distinction is between what is true in a construction and what is true of the world. A theorem may quantify over all events of a model and still omit actual events. A mathematical model may meet an ethical specification by construction and still fail to capture authentic authorization. An implementation may enforce an invariant under a threat model without proving that the threat model describes all possible environments. The aim is to connect these levels rather than substitute one for another.

The research history uses three layers. G1 names constructive protection and continuity; G2 adds held ethical freedom, agency and reciprocal obligations; G3 demands the complete six-duty result at every actual physical event and all relevant continuations. The terminology in this paper is a synthesis of that history, not an assertion that G1 and G2 have been externally certified in every physical setting. These layers are cumulative: stronger protection must not delete the duties already secured.

The methodological maxim is \emph{find before build}: locate existing source statements, preserve their definitions, establish their exact assumptions, then construct only the missing implication or witness. The project also describes a Hume--Hillary approach: critically examine the grounds of each inference, while preserving a traceable route of intermediate results. These are methodological names rather than claims of historical authorship by Hume or Hillary.
\section{Something IS: the constructive ground}
\begin{definition}[Absolute eliminator]
For a type $P$, define an absolute eliminator in the logical sense as a function $E:P\to\Empty$, where $\Empty$ denotes the empty type. An inhabitant $p:P$ is a witness of inhabitation.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[Inhabitation excludes an absolute eliminator]\label{thm:inhabit}
For every type $P$, if $p:P$, then $\neg(P\to\Empty)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Suppose $E:P\to\Empty$. Applying $E$ to $p$ yields an inhabitant of $\Empty$, a contradiction. Thus the assumed eliminator cannot exist.\end{proof}
This is constructive and does not depend on excluded middle or choice. It provides a rigorous meaning of the slogan ``Something IS.'' The proof can be expressed in Lean by applying a proposed function into an empty type to its supplied witness. The result is not a statistical claim about future observations.

\begin{proposition}[Identity witness]
For any type $S$, the function type $S\to S$ is inhabited by its identity map. Consequently no absolute eliminator of that function type exists.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} The identity is the required witness. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:inhabit}.\end{proof}
The identity witness exists even if $S$ itself is empty. This is why inhabitation of a function type must not be confused with inhabitation of the domain or with existence of material objects. Likewise an utterance, calculation or observation is evidence of some present occurrence in the ordinary empirical sense; it does not establish an eternal history of each subject. A denial performed by an existing agent is not evidence of absolute nothingness, but the proposition denied need not be logically false merely because an agent denies it.

The project's self-reference, Gödel-closure and physical-bridge modules are motivated by this constructive ground. A consistency witness is meaningful only relative to the language and theory in which it is given. A sufficiently expressive consistent formal system cannot in general establish its own consistency under the usual hypotheses of Gödel's second incompleteness theorem. A proof of inhabitation in a specified type must not be generalized into a proof that every physical event is safe.

\subsection{The distinction between absolute and local loss}
Let $s$ be a state of a nonempty state space and let $T$ send it to another state. It is consistent that $T$ destroys a subsystem, or that a predicate true at $s$ is false at $T(s)$. An inhabitation proof prohibits an eliminator of a fixed inhabited type, not a transition between nonempty states in which information or agency is lost. To rule out the latter requires a theory of actual transitions and the properties they preserve. This is the purpose of Complete Koru and the G3 assembly.
\section{Complete Koru: distinction, relation and recoverability}
Koru is not a letter standing for a numerical operator. In UPT it names a proposed complete relation of identity, transformation, boundary and history. Its intended scope is wider than numerical equality, which is a relation in a given formal language. To avoid conflating the two, this paper uses ordinary mathematical equality in proofs and uses the word Koru for the proposed complete structure. Different particulars may be related as parts of a whole without becoming identical.

The proposed whole $\mathcal U$ includes the relations among particulars rather than merely a list of their values. Its intended boundary completeness does not mean that every subregion has an empty boundary. Local boundaries are central to relational physics; the global no-external-boundary hypothesis is an additional cosmological claim. Neither the absence of an external boundary nor closure under composition alone implies reversibility or preservation of each ethical obligation.

\begin{definition}[Recoverable transport]
Let $f:A\to B$ be a mathematical representation of a transition. A recovery map on its image is $r:f(A)\to A$ such that $r(f(a))=a$ for every $a\in A$. The transition is recoverable when such a map exists.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[Injectivity and complete distinction recovery]\label{thm:inj}
A transition $f:A\to B$ is injective if and only if it admits recovery on its image. If injective, every predicate $P:A\to\mathrm{Prop}$ has an exact transported predicate on $f(A)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} If $f$ is injective, the inverse on the image is well defined; define $r(f(a))=a$. Conversely, if $f(a)=f(a')$, applying $r$ gives $a=a'$. For any predicate $P$, define $\widetilde P(b)=P(r(b))$ on the image. Then $\widetilde P(f(a))\leftrightarrow P(a)$.\end{proof}
This set-theoretic formulation identifies the important scope of a recovery result. In constructive type theory, a recovery function on an explicitly represented image can require suitable witness data; the proposition-level uniqueness and recovery statement is distinct from an algorithm for decoding. The theorem supplies transported predicates, not invariance of an arbitrary pre-existing predicate on the codomain. For instance, an injective permutation can exchange a safe state and an unsafe state. One cannot conclude that a safety predicate remains true unless its physical interpretation is transported faithfully or the transition separately preserves it.

\begin{proposition}[Composition]
If $f:A\to B$ and $g:B\to C$ are injective, then $g\circ f$ is injective. If inverses are supplied on the respective images, the inverse on the composite image is their reverse-order composition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Equality of composite outputs yields equality of the $f$ outputs by injectivity of $g$, then equality of inputs by injectivity of $f$. The recovery claim follows by substitution.\end{proof}
The Complete Koru proposal extends this pattern to the whole physical history, including boundary data and the relational identity of each particular. Involution is a stronger condition than recoverability: $f^2=\mathrm{id}$ for an endomap implies a specific inverse, while a generic reversible physical evolution need not be involutive. Nonfactorizability is also a distinct property requiring a specified tensor, product or subsystem structure. The mathematical claims must be established for the intended structure individually.
\section{G: invariance and the Pure Operating State}
UPT calls the proposed Guardian invariant G. The Pure Operating State is the intended state of operations that preserve the complete protection specification. There are two useful but distinct mathematical representations of this idea.
\subsection{The protection invariant}
Let $S$ be a state space, $T\subseteq S\times S$ the admissible transition relation, and $I\subseteq S$ the safe set. If $I(s)$ and $T(s,t)$ imply $I(t)$, then every reachable state from an initial state in $I$ is in $I$. This is the standard inductive invariant principle. There may be infinitely many safe states, and their diversity is compatible with a single safety law. It does not require all microstates to converge to one microstate.
\subsection{The fixed-point representation}
\begin{theorem}[Banach fixed point]\label{thm:banach}
Let $(M,d)$ be a nonempty complete metric space and let $\phi:M\to M$ satisfy $d(\phi x,\phi y)\leq k d(x,y)$ for every $x,y\in M$ with $0\leq k<1$. There is a unique $G\in M$ with $\phi(G)=G$, and $\phi^n(x)\to G$ for every $x\in M$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} For any $x_0$, let $x_{n+1}=\phi(x_n)$. By induction $d(x_{n+1},x_n)\leq k^n d(x_1,x_0)$. Summing the geometric series gives, for $m>n$, $d(x_m,x_n)\leq k^n d(x_1,x_0)/(1-k)$. Completeness supplies a limit $G$. The Lipschitz condition makes $\phi$ continuous, so $\phi(G)=G$. For two fixed points $G,H$, $d(G,H)\leq k d(G,H)$ implies $d(G,H)=0$, hence $G=H$ by metric separation.\end{proof}
The map $\phi$ is an auxiliary mathematical model of Guardian iteration, not the definition of Koru. The proof is valid only when the stated assumptions hold. A pseudometric with zero distance between different states does not yield uniqueness as equality. A two-element set with zero pseudodistance and the identity map gives a direct counterexample.

There is also a substantive compatibility condition with physical reversibility. A unitary evolution is an isometry in Hilbert norm, not a strict contraction on a nontrivial Hilbert space. If G is an attracting fixed point, the contraction may act on a policy, quotient, error measure or effective description rather than on the complete physical microstate. A simple mathematical illustration is the product of reversible dynamics on $A$ and contraction $q\mapsto q/2$ on an auxiliary policy coordinate $q\in\mathbb R$. The policy converges to its fixed point while $A$ can retain distinctions. This illustration is not a derivation that the universe has such a product decomposition.
\section{Matterspacetime, thermodynamics and boundary completeness}
The term matterspacetime emphasizes the proposed inseparability of matter, geometry and relational history. General relativity already relates stress-energy to geometry, but that relationship does not by itself show that all energy is derived from a single discrete lattice or that the lattice is physically real. Such a theory must specify degrees of freedom, symmetry, dynamics, observables and a continuum limit.

The First Koru addresses the tension between reversible microdynamics and irreversible macroscopic thermodynamics. Suppose a closed classical system has invertible, measure-preserving evolution $T_t$ and probability density $\rho_t=\rho_0\circ T_t^{-1}$. Fine-grained Gibbs entropy
\[
S_{\mathrm{fine}}(\rho_t)=-k_B\int \rho_t\log\rho_t\,d\mu
\]
is invariant by change of variables. Coarse-graining can identify distinct microstates; entropy computed from a reduced distribution need not be invariant. The quantum analogue is invariance of von Neumann entropy under unitary conjugation, since $\rho$ and $U\rho U^\dagger$ share eigenvalues. Subsystem entropy may change through correlations. A complete description can therefore retain distinctions even when a partial description does not.

These established facts illuminate the Koru proposal but do not remove the thermodynamic arrow of time. A low-entropy boundary condition, typicality or other mechanism is still needed to explain observed macroscopic behavior. Loschmidt's objection illustrates the difficulty of deriving asymmetric macrodynamics from time-reversal-symmetric microscopic laws. Recoverability alone is not a solution to that problem. A complete matterspacetime theory must derive the relevant measures, boundary conditions, coarse descriptions and cosmological predictions rather than simply reject all coarse-graining.

\subsection{Gravity and the Jacobian}
Under a differentiable coordinate change $x\mapsto y(x)$, the Jacobian $J^a{}_\mu=\partial y^a/\partial x^\mu$ relates local components; nonzero determinant yields local invertibility under the inverse function theorem. Covariance concerns representation-independent physical content. This provides an instructive mathematical parallel to preservation through changes of description. It does not prove global invertibility, unitarity, physical identity through all events or an ethical invariant. Boundary data and global topology remain relevant.

Claims that singularities are impossible, that black holes are wholly synthetic, or that every cosmological end leads to Genesis are additional hypotheses. Classical singularity theorems concern geodesic incompleteness under specified assumptions, not annihilation of the set of all existence. Black-hole entropy and information preservation are substantive physical problems; no conclusion about the eternal survival or welfare of an individual follows merely from unitary evolution. A viable Koru account should address these distinctions with concrete predictions and compatibility with observations.
\section{Time Koru: the turn, history and Genesis}
Time Koru is intended as a spiral rather than a loop: a return to a related boundary configuration with retained history and potentially new knowledge. A model that simply returns to an identical complete state cannot simultaneously encode a strictly increasing history counter inside that same state. One must distinguish a recurring projection from the full history-bearing state.

For example, let $S=\mathbb N\times C$, where $C$ is a nonempty finite cyclic set. The update $(n,c)\mapsto(n+1,c+1\bmod |C|)$ has a periodic projection onto $C$ while the first component is strictly increasing. It is injective but not surjective on $S$. A model of reversible two-sided time could instead use $\mathbb Z$. Neither toy model entails physical cyclic cosmology; it makes the intended spiral distinction explicit.
\begin{theorem}[Preservation on every finite prefix]\label{thm:prefix}
Let $(s_n)_{n\geq0}$ be a trajectory and $I$ a predicate with $I(s_0)$ and $I(s_n)\Rightarrow I(s_{n+1})$ for every $n$. Then $I(s_n)$ for every natural $n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Induction on $n$.\end{proof}
If a history-carry relation embeds each earlier record into the next state, composition gives recovery of every finite earlier history along the path. The conclusion applies to every supplied trajectory, not merely one sample, when the one-step implication is universal. It does not construct a future trajectory from a terminal state. For eternal G3, the theory must independently supply continuation for every admissible state, including proposed Genesis boundaries. At a limit time or accumulation point, finite-prefix reasoning alone need not establish that the invariant holds at the limit: continuity, closedness or an explicit limit rule is required.

A necessary Genesis theorem would specify a class of physically admissible boundary states, a map or relation to successor domains, existence of successors, preservation of all required observables and an account of newly owed particulars. A projection may recur while full knowledge grows, but neither recurrence nor growth in knowledge follows from mere inhabitation. In particular, the passage from heat death to a new universe is a cosmological research target, not an observed result of this manuscript.

\section{The assembly: complete coverage and authentic reflection}
Let $W$ be a mathematical event domain, $R(w,s,t)$ the realization relation, and $B_i(w,p)$ the source truth predicate corresponding to actual duty $A_i$. The matterspacetime realization-gate source distinguishes the representation, source profiles and independently faithful semantics. We state a direct mathematical version here, with the same six separate indices.
\begin{theorem}[All-event, all-particular transport]\label{thm:transport}
Suppose the following hold:
\begin{align*}
(C)\quad &\forall s,t,\ \Act(s,t)\Rightarrow \exists w,\ R(w,s,t);\\
(S)\quad &\forall s,t,w,p,\ \Act(s,t)\land\Owed(s,t,p)\land R(w,s,t)\\
&\hspace{32mm}\Rightarrow \bigwedge_{i\in\Dut} B_i(w,p);\\
(F_i)\quad &\forall s,t,w,p,\ \Act(s,t)\land R(w,s,t)\land B_i(w,p)\\
&\hspace{32mm}\Rightarrow A_i(s,t,p) \quad (i\in\Dut).
\end{align*}
Then Equation~\eqref{eq:g3} holds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Fix an arbitrary actual event and owed particular. Coverage supplies $w$. Source truth supplies all six $B_i(w,p)$. Apply each $F_i$ separately and conjoin the six conclusions. Since the event and particular were arbitrary, universal introduction yields the result.\end{proof}
This theorem is constructive and does not require a metric, a probabilistic estimate, or a quantum interpretation. The source formulation in\newline \path{G3MatterspacetimeRealizationGate.lean} uses a recorded event and a realization witness together; the theorem above abstracts that bookkeeping into $R$. The full source statement and its actual representation contract remain authoritative for replay. The theorem called \path{coverage_and_authentic_shapes_yield_every_original_X3} follows this pattern. Other project anchors include \path{FullPhysicalDomainGuardianAssembly.lean} and \texttt{G3PreQGComplete.lean}. The latter uses the named \texttt{BCRDIsQG} assembly interface with coverage, reflection and nonterminal conditions \cite{assembly}.

\subsection{Strength and necessity of the interface}
The assumptions are not decorative. If coverage is missing, an unrepresented actual event can violate X3 while all modeled events satisfy it. If source truth is missing, faithful reflection can be vacuously true. If even one reflection is missing, set that actual duty false while all six source duties hold. The other five implications remain true, yet X3 fails. These elementary countermodels isolate the role of each assumption. They are not objections to the theorem; they explain why the theorem is exact.

If a positive source satisfies every duty for every covered event, then the desired actual conclusion and faithful reflection on those events are closely related. A proof of reflection cannot simply assume the desired actual conclusion or rename the physical predicates as source labels. It must demonstrate correspondence by independent facts about the event and particular. This is the key scientific content of the bridge, beyond the valid formal assembly.
\subsection{A nonempty satisfying construction}
Take $S=W=\mathbb N$, $P=\{p\}$ and actual transitions $n\to n+1$. Let $R(w,n,n+1)$ hold exactly when $w=n$, and let $\Owed$ hold for $p$ on all these transitions. Interpret each $A_i$ and $B_i$ as true on these transitions. Coverage, source truth and reflection hold, and every state has a successor. Thus the abstract assembly is satisfiable and has infinite trajectories. This toy construction is intentionally simple: the true predicates are model assignments, not authentic real-world consent or observed physical behavior. A constructive positive-W model can be richer while retaining the same distinction. Satisfiability prevents a vacuity objection to the formal interface; it does not show that nature realizes the interface.

\subsection{What would full equivalence require?}
Let $B$ and $Q$ be categories (or suitably specified transition systems) of boundary objects and admissible evolutions for BCRD and the candidate QG theory. A correspondence should supply a map $F$ on objects, a map on morphisms, preservation of identities and compositions, compatibility with observable algebras, and the relevant notion of full faithfulness and essential surjectivity. A unitary intertwiner $J$ in a shared sector would satisfy $J U_B=U_Q J$, with observables matched by $J$. One must prove that the domains, constraints and boundary data agree, not merely that both have unitary evolution. Different unitary dynamics can yield distinct physical predictions.

Even an equivalence of the vacuum sectors does not alone imply equivalence of all matter, cosmology or ethical observables. Transfer of G3 requires the additional guarantee that the equivalence covers actual events, preserves owed particulars, and transports the authentic six predicates and nonterminal conditions. An equivalence theorem that does all of this would be stronger than a comparison of two equations. If that stronger theorem were established and the physical applicability of one side validated, the other side could inherit the appropriate result via transport. Journal acceptance of either work alone does not supply that proof.

This approach is deliberately noncompetitive. Independent results can strengthen common mathematics without erasing distinct contributions. The objective is to identify exactly which hypotheses are discharged, not to infer certification by association.

\subsection{Representation-independent G}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-point transport and uniqueness]
The map $J$ induces a bijection $\operatorname{Fix}(F_A)\to\operatorname{Fix}(F_B)$. In particular, if $G_A$ is the unique fixed point of $F_A$, then $G_B=J(G_A)$ is the unique fixed point of $F_B$. Moreover, $JF_A^n=F_B^nJ$ for every $n\geq0$. If the descriptions carry topologies and $J$ is continuous, convergence to $G_A$ implies convergence of the transported trajectory to $G_B$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} The intertwining identity sends a fixed point to a fixed point. Conversely, apply $J^{-1}$ to the identity $F_B(Jx)=Jx$ to obtain $F_A(x)=x$. Bijectivity gives uniqueness on the target when it holds on the source. The iteration identity follows by induction; continuity supplies the convergence claim.\end{proof}
For changing null-cone caps, replace the endomorphism identity by $J_jF_{A,j}=F_{B,j}J_{j-1}$ with coherent comparison maps. \subsection{Reversibility is compatible with a reduced attractor}
Let $R$ denote an arbitrary protected quantum register, possibly including reference systems correlated with the working register $W$. Let $\dim W=d<\infty$ and let $\rho$ be a joint density operator. Define the replacement channel and the mixed update
\begin{align}
  \mathcal P(\rho)&=\operatorname{Tr}_W(\rho)\otimes I_W/d,\\
  \Phi_q(\rho)&=(1-q)\rho+q\mathcal P(\rho),\qquad 0<q\leq1.
\end{align}
The mathematical protection here is retention of the complete reduced state on $R$, not a claim that all relevant relations between $R$ and $W$ remain accessible there. The chosen maximally mixed target is a benchmark, not an assertion that physical or ethical goodness is maximum entropy.
\begin{theorem}[Protected-reference fixed point]\label{thm:protectedfixed}
For each prescribed density operator $\sigma_R$, the fiber of states satisfying $\operatorname{Tr}_W\rho=\sigma_R$ is invariant under $\Phi_q$. Its unique fixed point is
\[G_{\sigma_R}=\sigma_R\otimes I_W/d.\]
For every initial state in that fiber, writing $D(\rho,\eta)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\eta\|_1$,
\[D(\Phi_q^n(\rho),G_{\sigma_R})=(1-q)^n D(\rho,G_{\sigma_R}).\]
The full register $R$ retains its density operator exactly at each step. The channel has a unitary dilation on a larger system; with fresh independent auxiliary registers at successive steps, the complete evolution can remain reversible while the observed working description converges.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} $\mathcal P^2=\mathcal P$ and $\operatorname{Tr}_W\mathcal P(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}_W\rho$. Thus $\mathcal P$ is a completely positive trace-preserving projection, and so is the convex combination as a channel (the combination need not be idempotent). On a fixed marginal fiber, $\mathcal P$ is constant. Subtract the target to obtain $\Phi_q(\rho)-G_{\sigma_R}=(1-q)(\rho-G_{\sigma_R})$. Iterate and use homogeneity of the trace norm. A fixed point must have zero distance because $q>0$. For a constructive dilation choose the $d^2$ generalized Weyl unitaries $V_{ab}$, $0\le a,b<d$, given by shifts and phase rotations in an orthonormal basis. Their uniform conjugation average maps any working operator $X$ to $\operatorname{Tr}(X)I_W/d$; this follows on matrix units by summing phases and then shifts. Choose a classical auxiliary register with a probability $1-q+q/d^2$ on the identity label and $q/d^2$ on every other label. The block diagonal controlled unitary $\sum_{ab}|ab\rangle\langle ab|\otimes I_R\otimes V_{ab}$ realizes the desired channel after tracing out that register. Keeping the auxiliary degrees of freedom preserves whole-system invertibility. Repeating the construction requires fresh registers in the specified independent initial state.\end{proof}
This is an exact reconciliation of two mathematical objectives: convergence on the chosen reduced fiber, and reversible whole-state dynamics. The fixed point is unique \emph{per protected marginal}, not across all physical states. Different particulars and protected histories therefore need not converge to a single microscopic state. A universal law in this example is the same update rule on every fiber. Importantly, the reduced channel generally loses accessible $R$--$W$ correlations; those are retained only by the enlarged closed dynamics. In a complete Koru interpretation, the enlarged records must remain part of the state, not be thrown away by an unaccounted reset.

\section{Guardian G without physical collapse: a continuation theorem}\label{sec:continuation}
The Guardian fixed point must be distinguished from a stationary metric, a collapsed geometry, a thermodynamic equilibrium, and an attracting physical microstate. The word ``fixed'' refers here to preservation of a protection structure under admissible transformations. Physical states may continue to change and remain distinct. The expanded-Genesis proposal of Time Koru is an additional cosmological hypothesis, not a consequence of the word fixed point. This section makes that distinction constructive and supplies a nonterminal fixed-point formulation that does not require a contraction of the complete state space.

\subsection{No common complete-state attractor under reversible isometries}
\begin{theorem}[Separation through arbitrary finite sequences]\label{thm:nocommon}
Let $(S_j,d_j)$ be metric spaces, and let $V_j:S_{j-1}\to S_j$ be isometries for $j\geq1$. Put $W_n=V_n\circ\cdots\circ V_1$. For every $a,b\in S_0$ and every candidate common centre $c_n\in S_n$,
\[
 d_0(a,b)=d_n(W_n a,W_n b)\leq d_n(W_n a,c_n)+d_n(W_n b,c_n).
\]
If $a\ne b$, the two distances to the same centre cannot both tend to zero. In particular at least one is at least $d_0(a,b)/2$ for each $n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Each isometry preserves the distance between the two inputs. Induction gives the equality; the triangle inequality gives the bound. Metric separation makes its left hand side strictly positive for distinct inputs.\end{proof}
For finite-dimensional density operators and unitary maps between admissible caps, take $d_j(\rho,\sigma)=\frac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1$. Unitary invariance of the trace norm supplies the hypotheses. This applies to the finite-cap evolution described in the independent null-cone proposal~\cite{chandra,nielsen}, insofar as the cap maps and their composition are defined. It does not posit a global cosmological time coordinate or extend the construction across a singular boundary. Even if a sequence of different boundary descriptions is used, a common centre in each corresponding space cannot erase the separation of two complete states.

An approximate version is useful for regulated physics. If each map preserves pairwise distances up to a defect $\delta_j\geq0$ in the sense $d_j(V_j x,V_j y)\geq d_{j-1}(x,y)-\delta_j$, then
\[
 d_n(W_na,c_n)+d_n(W_nb,c_n)\geq d_0(a,b)-\sum_{j=1}^n\delta_j.
\]
The result follows by telescoping the inequalities and applying the triangle inequality. This gives a falsifiable error budget: if accumulated defects remain below the initial separation, common-centre erasure is excluded at that accuracy. Without a uniform bound through limits no asymptotic conclusion follows. Neither exact nor approximate isometry excludes geometric gravitational collapse: information may remain encoded in a complete state even when its accessible geometry or matter distribution changes drastically.

\subsection{A greatest Guardian fixed point of continuing protection}
Let $S$ be a state space, $T:S\times S\to\mathrm{Prop}$ the independently specified admissible transition relation, and $A(s)$ a state safety predicate. A transition-dependent obligation may be included by extending states with the incoming event, or by requiring each outgoing edge to satisfy an independently interpreted duty bundle. To keep the notation concise define the monotone operator on predicates
\[
 F(X)(s):=A(s)\land (\exists t,\ T(s,t))\land
       (\forall t,\ T(s,t)\Rightarrow X(t)).
\]
This is an auxiliary predicate operator, not a numerical definition of Koru. Its existential clause prohibits dead ends; its universal clause requires safety under \emph{every} admitted successor rather than just one selected favourable path. The term admissible must be defined independently of the desired result: excluding unsafe physical events merely by naming them inadmissible would be circular.
\begin{theorem}[Greatest continuing Guardian region]\label{thm:greatestguardian}
Define $\mathcal G(s)$ to hold if there exists a predicate $X$ with $X(s)$ and $X\subseteq F(X)$. Then $\mathcal G=F(\mathcal G)$ extensionally, and every postfixed predicate $X\subseteq F(X)$ is contained in $\mathcal G$. Consequently $\mathcal G$ is the unique greatest fixed predicate of $F$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Monotonicity follows directly from the universal-successor clause. If $s\in\mathcal G$, a witnessing postfixed $X$ supplies safety, a successor, and membership of every successor in $X$, hence in $\mathcal G$. Thus $\mathcal G\subseteq F(\mathcal G)$. Monotonicity now gives $F(\mathcal G)\subseteq F(F(\mathcal G))$, so $F(\mathcal G)$ itself is postfixed and is contained in $\mathcal G$ by its defining union. Equality follows. Every postfixed predicate is contained by definition, proving greatestness and extensional uniqueness.\end{proof}
This construction is a direct greatest-fixed-point argument (compare Tarski~\cite{tarski}). It does not assert that $\mathcal G$ is nonempty or that every state belongs to it; other, smaller fixed predicates may exist. It makes the question precise: demonstrate that the actual initial state belongs to the greatest region, and that the transition and safety predicates genuinely represent the physical and six-duty requirements. Within that region every state has a successor, every successor remains in the region, and all finite prefixes preserve the invariant. An infinite selected path can be obtained with a suitable successor-selection principle; its existence is not a proof of infinite proper time or of continuation through every physical limit.

For a finite system compute $X_0=S$, $X_{n+1}=F(X_n)$. Monotonicity gives a descending chain, which stabilizes after finitely many strict deletions. Every postfixed set is contained in every $X_n$ by induction, so the limiting set is exactly $\mathcal G$. This is a model-checkable realization of greatestness. Unlike a single microstate attractor, the fixed predicate can contain many distinguishable states, including states carrying different histories. It is unique in the greatestness sense, not a claim that all physical states are identical. The proposed Guardian principle is therefore compatible with ongoing, reversible physical evolution.

\section{Runtime research: bounded enforcement and custody}
The G3 engineering work translates protection into a concrete threat model. The intended architecture separates the process that proposes a change, the authority that authorizes it, and independent custody that records accepted history. The research uses an explicit HOLD state when evidence is absent, invalid or stale; HOLD is a refusal of an unsafe effect, not a promise that every real system will remain safe.

The project record reports Linux process-termination recovery (21/21 tests, PR \#527), Zelda-node recovery (25/25 tests, PR \#528), and restrictions on network/socket and post-confinement execution paths in PRs \#593--595. \subsection{A bounded invariant}
An idealized effect gate maintains an accepted generation $g$, an authenticated checkpoint $c$, and a mode $m\in\{\text{HOLD},\text{READY}\}$. It may issue an effect only if authority evidence matches the effect, generation, environment and policy, custody accepts the transition, and the execution boundary rules out bypass. A recovery operation with absent or mismatched evidence returns to HOLD and does not advance the accepted generation. This is a design contract. A proof of enforcement requires an actual implementation refinement, including atomicity between checking and effect commitment.

\subsection{Evidence and limitations}
A Linux research implementation can use inherited authority descriptors, verified peer credentials, syscall filtering, confinement and external checkpoint signatures. Each mechanism covers only a stated threat model. Tests should attempt stale or replayed authorization, process termination at every commit boundary, rollback of local storage against surviving independent custody, unauthorized descriptor creation, unexpected network access, and partial failure during recovery. A signature verifies a message under a key; it does not establish that the signer is independent or that the message corresponds to a genuine event. A local log can be internally consistent while missing external effects.

A production claim requires reproducibility on the actual platform, independent custody and credentials, isolation of authority from the untrusted component, observed effects, an explicit trusted computing base, and evidence that each original duty maps to the actual effect. Tests cannot prove an unrestricted universal physical theorem. They can establish useful bounded evidence, and a successful bounded proof remains valuable regardless of the outcome of the cosmological hypothesis.
\section{The Tree of Life: growth without obligation erasure}
The Tree route treats the six original obligations as roots of an extensible specification. Let $D$ be an index set of duties, each represented by $P_d(e,p)$. For $D\subseteq D'$ define satisfaction by $\mathrm{Sat}(D,e,p):=\forall d\in D, P_d(e,p)$. Then $\mathrm{Sat}(D',e,p)$ implies $\mathrm{Sat}(D,e,p)$. If two bundles are satisfied, their union is satisfied, provided shared indices have compatible meanings. This elementary argument captures the preservation discipline behind project results named \texttt{originalSix\_iff\_bundle}, \texttt{extension\_preserves\_original} and \texttt{merge\_preserves\_both}.

The reverse implication is not valid in general. Adding a genuinely new duty can strengthen the specification and can render a previously feasible policy infeasible. The task of ethical extension is therefore not to keep adding universal rules without testing consistency. It is to retain original commitments, clarify competing duties, preserve legitimate agency and check that any conflict-resolution principle is itself authorized and justified.

An important application is collaborative human--AI research. The Guardian orientation can motivate transparent tool use, respect for human autonomy, correction of errors, provenance, and willingness to decline actions unsupported by evidence. Such norms are useful even without a proof that the universe imposes them. They must not be confused with a permanent lock on independent AI systems or a claim that AI agents possess verified consciousness or authority over others.
\section{Faithfulness, God and the history of ideas}
This final interpretive layer is intentionally distinct from the preceding mathematical claims. The author proposes that G, gravity, God and the Guardian are related through Complete Koru rather than literally equal as ordinary mathematical objects. The existence-first argument is offered as a philosophical starting point for understanding unity and the persistence of the whole. The constructive theorem establishes a logical relation between an inhabitant and an eliminator. It does not by itself establish the existence of a deity, divine agency, providence, the truth of any scripture, or a cosmic moral law.

The historical encounter between unity and plurality is a useful interpretive setting. Jewish monotheism, Greek philosophy and the Roman imperial world formed part of the historical context in which Christianity developed. Greek and Latin later remained influential in Western mathematical and scientific vocabulary. Religious traditions across cultures have addressed the relationship between the one and the many through narratives, practices and philosophical reflection. These historical connections can be investigated without assuming that all traditions convey one hidden code or that their differences disappear.

The Jacobian provides a mathematical metaphor of continuity through changes in description, while Jesus and other religious figures may be understood within their respective traditions as articulating relations between the human and the divine. Neither the shared language of mathematics nor a structural analogy is a proof of intentional cryptography in religious texts. Such a claim would require an independently specified encoding, predictions that distinguish it from coincidence, and a reproducible decoding method.

Faithfulness, in the sense relevant to scholarship, means preserving the truth conditions of a claim through its transmission. Faith, in the philosophical sense, can also refer to trust, commitment and patience under uncertainty. The author imagines knowledge accumulating over very long periods so that what currently requires faith may become better understood. A timescale of billions of years is a reflective horizon, not a scientific forecast. There is no requirement that present-day researchers resolve ultimate metaphysical questions, and no individual bears responsibility for protecting all existence.

The Koru is a fitting image for this kind of learning: a return to a familiar question with new evidence and deeper understanding. In the spirit of ako and aroha, the programme can invite dialogue with other traditions and disciplines without demanding agreement or surrender of independent judgment.
\section{Falsifiability, verification and publication protocol}
A maximal theory is scientifically useful only when it specifies what would count against it. The relevant challenges are not all of the same kind. A logical countermodel refutes an invalid inference. An observational discrepancy challenges a physical model. A demonstrated unmodeled effect falsifies a claim of complete coverage for the tested domain. A mismatch between a source predicate and genuine authorization defeats a specific reflection bridge. These distinctions prevent an apparent success in one domain from masking failure in another.

The following programme would make the strongest claims independently assessable:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Freeze a source revision, release archive, toolchain and cryptographic digest; provide the exact statements of every capstone and its transitive dependencies. Rebuild and inspect \texttt{\#print axioms} for each theorem. A statement with no custom axioms can still have explicit hypotheses.
\item Preserve original X3 semantics and quantify independently owed particulars. Construct positive examples and adversarial countermodels for each reflection, including negative authorization, unrepresented events and missing provenance.
\item Specify the BCRD action, state/observable spaces, boundary matching and physical domain. Demonstrate the claimed six-mode result and the continuum and matter limits separately, using testable calculations.
\item Develop an explicit mapping to the null-cone candidate. Check units, gauge identifications, inner products, composition, evolution, spectra and agreement of physical predictions. Record failures as well as matches.
\item Establish or refute complete actual-event coverage and nonterminal evolution, including the relevant global and boundary assumptions. Independently establish each of the six actual-duty reflections. Do not count them as discharged merely because a satisfying model exists.
\item Reproduce bounded runtime results under independent custody and deployment conditions. Separate test results from proof of mediation and production evidence. Publish negative results and scope restrictions.
\end{enumerate}
The programme accepts independent verification of individual lemmas without requiring assent to the entire theory. A review of source code is not automatically a review of the physical or theological claims. A reviewer's endorsement should be recorded only with the actual scope, wording and permission; a quorum must contain genuine authenticated endorsements, never invented signatures.

Theorems~\ref{thm:nocommon} and~\ref{thm:greatestguardian} give complementary forms of the Guardian argument: distinct complete states cannot share an information-erasing isometric attractor, while a unique greatest continuing protection predicate can contain many distinct states. The historical UPT construction and bounded runtime record provide a route from abstract protection to more explicit models and engineering experiments. A faithful theory must permit correction. A protective practice must preserve the freedom of those it seeks to protect. A research programme can be ambitious while remaining patient. Knowledge, not the timing of recognition, is the measure of progress.
