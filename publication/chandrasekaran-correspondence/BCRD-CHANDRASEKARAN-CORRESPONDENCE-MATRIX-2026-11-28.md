## 8. The expanded vs. collapsed singularity distinction

This is the decisive point for the Guardian argument:

**Collapsed singularity (E-pattern):** All distinct states converge to a single identical state. The `no_common_output` theorem (GuardianContinuation.lean) and Theorem 10 of the manuscript prove this is impossible under isometric/unitary evolution. Chandrasekaran's unitarity (Eq. 4.18) independently confirms this impossibility in the gravitational sector.

**Expanded singularity (G-pattern):** The greatest Guardian fixed *predicate* `𝒢 = F(𝒢)` (Theorem 11 of the manuscript; `guardian_fixed` in GuardianContinuation.lean) contains many distinct states. History accumulates. No state merges with another. The protection law is unique as a predicate, universal as a region, and compatible with continued distinct evolution.

**The bridge:** Chandrasekaran's unitary evolution guarantees that the physical substrate preserves distinctions (no collapse). The Guardian fixed-point theorem then says: within that distinction-preserving dynamics, there is a unique greatest predicate of continuing protection. If the initial state belongs to that region, every successor does too.
