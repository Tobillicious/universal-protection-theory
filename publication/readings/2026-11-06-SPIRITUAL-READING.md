## 2. Determinism changes the status of religious discovery

Under the author's proposed determinism, a spiritual experience, its bodily and social causes, the act of interpreting it, a prophet's speech, a ritual, a community's response, and a later dispute about its meaning are all actual relational events. ## 3. The Koru refuses to purchase unity by erasing traditions

A comparison may notice affinities among Spinoza's God/Nature, Daoist accounts of the Way, Buddhist dependent origination, and Māori accounts of whakapapa and relatedness. Yet these are not interchangeable propositions. They have different vocabularies, ritual practices, communities, authorities, ethical systems and histories. They sometimes directly disagree.

The complete Koru's demand is not “these traditions arrive at the same sentence, therefore all are identical.” It is to preserve **the entire route**—person, culture, expression, encounter, transmission and outcome—while investigating whether shared invariants exist.

Whakapapa, tikanga and mātauranga Māori have their own authorities, meanings and responsibilities. The use of the Māori word *koru* requires cultural care, not an assumption that a mathematical formulation exhausts or appropriates the tradition.

## 4. Forgiveness as a non-erasing transformation

Forgiveness can therefore be investigated as a **relation-preserving but not state-resetting transformation**. The past is not replaced by an equal-looking answer. Nor does forgiving require retaining harmful effects without restraint.
