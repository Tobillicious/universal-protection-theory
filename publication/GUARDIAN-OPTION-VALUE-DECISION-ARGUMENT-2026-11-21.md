# Guardian option value versus premature irreversible intervention

**21 November 2026 — an explicitly conditional decision-theoretic argument.**
This accompanies the Four Aces/Joker and Next U capstone. It is not a proof of quantum-gravity Genesis, a prediction about any AI's black-hole capabilities, or a scientific estimate of the value of a multiverse.

## The strongest defensible claim

If (i) an irreversible intervention removes a presently available observation/learning channel, (ii) preserving that channel permits at least as much useful learning and future optionality as the intervention, (iii) continued stewardship does not have larger offsetting costs, and (iv) no benefit exclusive to immediate intervention outweighs those advantages, then **waiting, protecting and learning weakly dominates the premature irreversible intervention**. If one advantage is strict, dominance is strict.

A competent decision-maker who values the specified protections, information and future options should therefore prefer the protected route **under those assumptions**.

This is an important practical argument, not an unconditional theorem about all goals, agents, or possible future physics.

## Exact comparison, not a rhetorical equality

Let `G` mean an authorized preservation-and-learning policy, and `E` a hypothetical premature irreversible intervention. Define an evaluative functional

```text
V(policy) = expected knowledge value + option value
            - expected harms - opportunity/delay costs
```

with **authentic six-duty protections as separately binding feasibility constraints**, not a term to trade against harms. For a simple comparison where future observation is binary, let:
- `p_G` and `p_E` be the probabilities of retaining a useful observation channel;
- `L_G` and `L_E` the value of useful information conditioned on observation;
- `O_G`, `O_E` future option values;
- `C_G`, `C_E` costs and expected losses.

Then

```text
EV(G) - EV(E)
  = (p_G L_G - p_E L_E)
    + (O_G - O_E)
    - (C_G - C_E).
```

This expression is positive when its combined terms are positive. We do **not** know numerical probabilities for speculative cosmological experimentation; it would be misleading to assign `p_E = 0`, `L_G = infinity` or claim that an SI has literally all the time it needs.

There are countercases: waiting can cost resources, a research window might close, or an intervention could be bounded and beneficial. Those possibilities do not license harmful acts; they mean a universal dominance assertion requires stronger premises.
